# Gospel

## Luke 24.13-35 of Sunday April 19, 2026

On the first day of the week, two of the disciples were going to a village called Emmaus, about eleven kilometres from Jerusalem, and talking with each other about all these things that had happened. While they were talking and discussing, Jesus himself came near and went with them, but their eyes were kept from recognizing him.  
  
And he said to them, “What are you discussing with each other while you walk along?” They stood still, looking sad. Then one of them, whose name was Cleopas, answered him, “Are you the only stranger in Jerusalem who does not know the things that have taken place there in these days?”  
  
He asked them, “What things?” They replied, “The things about Jesus of Nazareth, who was a Prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people, and how our chief priests and leaders handed him over to be condemned to death and crucified him. But we had hoped that he was the one to redeem Israel. Yes, and besides all this, it is now the third day since these things took place. Moreover, some women of our group astounded us. They were at the tomb early this morning, and when they did not find his body there, they came back and told us that they had indeed seen a vision of Angels who said that he was alive. Some of those who were with us went to the tomb and found it just as the women had said; but they did not see him.”  
  
Then he said to them, “Oh, how foolish you are, and how slow of heart to believe all that the Prophets have declared! Was it not necessary that the Christ should suffer these things and then enter into his glory?”  
  
Then beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them the things about himself in all the Scriptures. As they came near the village to which they were going, he walked ahead as if he were going on. But they urged him strongly, saying, “Stay with us, because it is almost evening and the day is now nearly over.” So he went in to stay with them.  
  
When he was at the table with them, he took bread, blessed and broke it, and gave it to them. Then their eyes were opened, and they recognized him; and he vanished from their sight.  
  
They said to each other, “Were not our hearts burning within us while he was talking to us on the road, while he was opening the Scriptures to us?”  
  
That same hour they got up and returned to Jerusalem; and they found the eleven and their companions gathered together. These were saying, “The Lord has risen indeed, and he has appeared to Simon!”  
  
Then they told what had happened on the road, and how he had been made known to them in the breaking of the bread.


# Homily

- Today's Gospel, often referred to as The Walk to Emmaus, is a very rich passage that can have meaning to us in many different ways and in many different contexts
	- It's one of my favorite passages
	- I have heard it used for reflection in different reflection contexts and have even used it myself as a topic for reflection for those working through the grief of losing a loved one
	- And when looked at from each of our own individual perspectives, we can very easily focus on different aspects of the passage that others might not relate to

- As I was reflecting on this passage in preparation for today though, I had the thought that each of us here has in our own way, taken the walk to Emmaus
	- What do I mean by that?
	- let's break this down and I'll explain my thoughts

- Lets break this Gospel down into 4 parts
	- The Walk, The Word, The Meal, The Return

- This Gospel passage start by telling us about two disciples who have left Jerusalem for Emmaus after Jesus has been crucified and has died on the cross. 
	- Let's call this part The Walk
	- The two disciples in the passage had left Jerusalem after Jesus had been crucified and the tomb was found empty on the third day
	- They believed Jesus to be the Messiah that had been long awaited for
	- However, the vision of the Messiah they imagined was one who would liberate them from the oppression they were under
		- a Messiah who would be their King
		- a Messiah who would cleanse the Temple of corruption and restore it to true holiness
	- All their hopes were broken by the current circumstances
	- All they could see were problems
	- And this is when Jesus comes up to them and joins them on their walk, although their eyes were kept from recognizing Him

- This is where the part of the Gospel that I call the Word begins
	- Jesus proceeds to tell them that they are being foolish, how they are not thinking about all the Prophets had declared would happen
	- Jesus proceeds to interpret and explain to them all that the Prophets had spoken about Jesus
	- Especially for Christ to have suffered all that He had, to be persecuted, to be crucified, to die on the cross and the be buried in the tomb
	- Jesus explains these prophecies, reminding the disciples of the things Jesus had also taught before His crucifixion
	- Although Jesus had taught them these prophecies before, though they did not understand then fully

- As they continue walk and Jesus moves ahead of them, as if to continue on in His own journey, the disciples strongly urged Jesus to stay with them as the day was nearly over and it was time to eat
	- This part of the Gospel, let's call it the Meal
	- Jesus stayed with them
	- and when Jesus took the bread, blessed it and broke it and gave it to them
	- this is when their eyes were opened
	- this is when they recognized Him, they see the Risen Christ
	- and this is when Jesus vanished from their sight

- The last part of the Gospel is called The Return
	- The disciples, their eyes opened both by The Word and by The Meal are filled with zeal
	- their hearts are burning with passion from Jesus opening the Scriptures to them
	- and with the Meal, this is how they recognize Jesus
	- They decide to return to Jerusalem
	- they don't wait til the morning, they leave right away
	- and remember that detail, that it was an 11 kilometer journey?
	- but that is the power that hope has in our lives
	- hope gives us the ability to take on the difficult things on our lives

- Now, how do these 4 parts of the Gospel relate to us and our own lives?
	- Each time, when we come to Mass, we are in essence, each of us, embarking on our own walk to Emmaus
	- Here's how I see it

- The Walk is our day to day life
	- we all have challenges in our lives, some big, some small
	- but we all have challenges
	- so like the disciples on the walk to Emmaus, we are all on our own journey, working through our own challenges
	- sometimes our challenges make it difficult to have hope
	- sometimes we are stuck by our challenges because we don't know the way forward
		- one piece of wisdom I figured out though, is we don't always have to have the complete solution to our challenges figured out
		- sometimes, we just need to figure out the next step in our journey
		- with time, we will figure the rest out
	- as you walk into this Church, how many of you are thinking of the challenges you have on your plate?
	- how many of you are you taking those challenges to Jesus
	- God wants to walk with us on our journey like He did with the disciples
	- like the disciples, we don't always recognize Him, but He is always there

- The Word is when we listen to the Scriptures at Mass
	- The readings and the Gospel is shared with us at Mass
	- And as Deacon's and Priests, we strive to unpack a message from the Scripture, a message to help us to have hope and understanding on our path, similar to what Jesus did with the disciples
	- and while none of us could do this quite as well as Jesus, we are blessed at St. Benedict to have a variety of perspectives by our clergy to unpack the Scripture and share God's message with you
	- and our hope is that we can inspire you much in the same way that Jesus inspired the disciples

- The Meal is when we celebrate the Eucharist
	- a gift that Jesus gave to us before he ascended to heaven to return to His Father's Right Hand
	- in the breaking of the bread, in the receiving of the Eucharist, we receive Jesus
	- with the disciples, this was when their eyes were opened and they recognized Jesus
	- do we see Jesus in our lives?  Do we need our eyes opened to be able to see Him?
	- God can open our eyes, so that we can see Him in our lives, the blessings that we have
	- the Risen Christ opens our eyes to hope, to see new perspectives and paths in our lives
	
- The Return is when we leave Mass
	- with our spirits renewed by the Word of God and strengthened by the Eucharist, we leave Mass, taking Jesus with us into the world to help us through the challenges we face, to continue His mission, to be Jesus for others in our lives

- So as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us think about
	- our own Walk to Emmaus
	- the Walk, the Word, the Meal, the Return
	- how the Mass, the Word and the Eucharist strengthens us, opens our eyes to the Risen Christ, inspires us to move forward in our lives, bringing Jesus with us on our walk of life
