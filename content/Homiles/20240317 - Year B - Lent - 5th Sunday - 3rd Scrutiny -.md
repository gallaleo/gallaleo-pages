# Gospel  
### John 12.20-33 of Sunday March 17, 2024

Among those who went up to worship at the festival were some Greeks. They came to Philip, who was from Bethsaida in Galilee, and said to him, “Sir, we wish to see Jesus.” Philip went and told Andrew; then Andrew and Philip went and told Jesus. Jesus answered them, “The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. Very truly, I tell you, unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains just a single grain; but if it dies, it bears much fruit. The person who loves their life loses it, and the person who hates their life in this world will keep it for eternal life.  
  
“Whoever serves me must follow me, and where I am, there will my servant be also. Whoever serves me, the Father will honour.  
  
“Now my soul is troubled. And what should I say — ‘Father, save me from this hour’? No, it is for this reason that I have come to this hour. Father, glorify your name.”  
  
Then a voice came from heaven, “I have glorified it, and I will glorify it again.”  
  
The crowd standing there heard it and said that it was thunder. Others said, “An Angel has spoken to him.” Jesus answered, “This voice has come for your sake, not for mine. Now is the judgment of this world; now the ruler of this world will be driven out. And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself.”  
  
Jesus said this to indicate the kind of death he was to die.

# Homily

- These words from Henri Nouwen resonated with me a lot when I read them this week
- While optimism makes us live as if someday soon things will soon go better for us, 
	- hope frees us from the need to predict the future and allows us to live in the present, 
	- with the deep trust that God will never leave us alone but will fulfill the deepest desires of our heart... 
	- Joy in this perspective is the fruit of hope.”
- I've always described myself as what I would call a pragmatic optimist.  I'm optimistic, but with a dose of reality added in
	- I am also a person who chooses to search for that which gives me hope in my life to justify that optimism
	- I always try to move forward in my life, even if I encounter situations that challenge me

- As a Deacon, all of my service is volunteer
	- Serving and preaching at Mass, the time it takes to write a Homily, Baptisms, my Ministry of Service
	- Which means I also have a full time day job to pay the bills and support my family
	- The company I work for has a set of values to help guide us in our day to day work and in the decisions we need to make as a part of our jobs
	- A couple of those values are "Obsess about our customers", "Think Big" and "Own It".
	- But one that really stands out for me is "Share Your Humanity"
	- Some of the points they use to describe this value is that as employees, 
		- we should "embrace our diversity", "be kind", "we are in this together" and "have some fun"
	- To me, and a lot of people I have talked to, it also means that we are human and we have good days and we have off days
	- Essentially, we should do our best, but we are not perfect.  We will have days where else in our life takes our focus away from our work
	- And that is ok
	- If we recognize that we are all in it together, that we help each other when we need it, then we will work our way through those off days and bounce back to our normal self

- Looking at today's Gospel, The words that jumped out for me were:  "Jesus wept"
	- One of the shortest sentences in the Bible.  Just two words.
	- But think about that.  "Jesus wept!!"
	- When we think about Jesus, this is not the first or the second or the third thing that comes to mind
	- We think about His good works
		- His knowing what to do in each situation
		- His healing powers
		- His prayerful ways
	- We don't think of Jesus as someone who would cry
	- Sharing in our humanity, Jesus wept with grief for his friend Lazarus

- Jesus became man, and experienced the ways of our human nature, including grief
	- Jesus has a human nature and a divine nature
	- He is fully human and He is fully God
	- In today's Gospel though, we see a part of His human side that we don't normally see
	- Lazarus, Mary and Martha were friends of Jesus.
	- And Jesus grieved over the death of Lazarus
	- He wept for the pain that Lazarus endured by dying
	- He wept knowing that it is due to sin in the world, passed down from the time of Adam and Eve that we have to experience sin and death in our lives

- Often in today's world though, it seems that crying is seen as a sign of weakness
	- However tears can be healing and cleansing for us
	- It is natural, and a way for us to release emotional tension, to help us feel better
	- And it is a normal response when we experience emotions like grief
	- After all, are not tears just water?

- Water is the primary symbol used in the Baptism Rite.
	- It's not the only one, but it is the one everyone remembers and thinks about
	- Water is key to life. 
	- We are all made up primarily of water and we can't live without it.
	- Water sustains all life on earth; it quenches thirst; it cleanses and refreshes.
	- It is a symbol of purification and of life.
	- Water is a sign of the life of God without whom we could not live.

- In our Baptism, we are cleansed from Original Sin
	- We die to our sinful ways and are born into a new life as children of God
	- So in a way, we are resurrected as children of God
	- Many of our RCIA candidates will be baptized at the Easter Vigil, taking the next step in their faith journey

- We are coming close to the end of Lent.  Next Sunday is Palm Sunday and the weekend after that, the Easter Triduum.
	- At this point in the Gospel, Jesus is also coming closer to His time of trial
	- The chief priests and Pharisees are feeling threatened by Jesus and are looking for a way to deal with Him

- Even though Jesus experiences grief at the loss of Lazarus, He still follows the path His Father has put in front of Him
	- Although Lazarus has been dead in the tomb for 4 days, Jesus trusts in the Father's path and has them open the tomb
	- He then calls Lazarus to emerge from the tomb, to return to life from death
	- Resurrecting Lazarus from the dead prefigures the trial that Jesus Himself will soon endure.
	- That of the Crucifixion and being Resurrected from the dead.
	- Again, Jesus will share in our human ways and experience death.

- But therein lies our hope!!
	- Jesus through His death and His resurrection, conquers death.
	- Jesus has walked our path, shared in humanity, but also made a way for our salvation
	- Our hope is that death is not the end.  
	- That there is hope for eternal life after death
	- That we will have a future with Jesus in eternity.

- We earn this salvation through our own actions
	- We all have a choice to make, a choice to follow Jesus's example.
	- To follow the path that God has put in front of us.
	- And that is one of God's greatest gifts to us
	- That of choice.
	- We don't have to follow that path
	- But through our faith, if we choose to follow that path, we have the hope of attaining eternal life

- So as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us think
	- about our human ways, and how Jesus not only experienced them, but experienced them without sin
	- about how we have to make our own choice, to follow Jesus' example
	- about how through Jesus' sacrifice, we have hope for our own salvation
