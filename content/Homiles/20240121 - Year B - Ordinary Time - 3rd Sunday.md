# Gospel  
### Mark 1.14-20 of Sunday January 21, 2024

After John was arrested, Jesus came to Galilee, proclaiming the good news of God, and saying, “The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news.”  
  
As Jesus passed along the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon and his brother Andrew casting a net into the sea — for they were fishermen. And Jesus said to them, “Come follow me and I will make you fishers of people.” And immediately they left their nets and followed him.  
  
As Jesus went a little farther, he saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John, who were in their boat mending the nets. Immediately he called them; and they left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men, and followed him.

# Homily

- I heard this quote.  "Life is a game.  You can't undo a move, but you can make the next one better!"
- And maybe that's why people make New Year's Resolutions, wanting to make the future better.
	- I haven't set New Years resolutions in years, instead I created goals to focus on for the year.
	- But this year, a friend told me about the practice of setting intentions instead of goals and this got me curious to understand the difference
- A simple explanation of what I found is this:
	- A resolution is a firm decision to do or not to do something
		- like this year I am going to go to the gym 3 times a week
	- A goal is a desired result, something you can measure
		- when you hit the goal, you have succeeded
		- like this year, I am going to lose 10 lbs
	- An intention is more about focusing on how you want to be and doing the things that help you do that.
		- like I will be someone who eats healthy and exercises
- This idea of setting intentions resonated with me and is what I chose to do this year
	- The thing I liked about intentions is that to me, they identify the person I want to be and the intentions I set for this year are really a set of priorities in my life.  Priorities that I think will help me on my journey
	- the best way I have found that explains the benefits of intentions is this:
			- First, you set an intention
			- Then it becomes a behaviour
			- Then a habit
			- Then a practice
			- Then it becomes second nature
			- Then it becomes simply a part of who you are
	- So, for me, the intentions I created for myself are there to guide me to become the person I want to be.
	- And I plan to use these intentions to help me make choices in my life

- we typically choose resolutions, goals or intentions when you have a desire to make changes in your life, to make your life better
	- and with any change that you make, making the choice to change is the first step in the process
	- with any choice though, there can be some, or even a lot of uncertainty involved, as a result of that choice
	- uncertainty on how that choice will affect your future
	- is it the right choice?
	- will I succeed in making the change I want to make?
	- and with any choice that you make, because there is some uncertainty, you need to have faith that you can succeed

- In today's Gospel, similar to last week, we hear about Jesus calling the first of his disciples.
	- He calls Simon Peter and his brother Andrew and James and his brother John
	- All four of them were fisherman.
	- Jesus says "Come follow me and I will make you fishers of people"
	- I've heard this Gospel more than a few times over the years
	- But lets stop and think about this from a different perspective
	- Let's think about this from the perspective of the choice that the disciples had to make

- The choice that Jesus puts in front of these first disciples seems like a pretty big choice
	- leave the work that you are doing, the work that puts food on the table and supports your household
	- to go do something that they likely didn't have a full understanding of what was involved
	- they didn't know the many trials they would face as a part of their discipleship
	- but there had to have been some uncertainty
	- like Jesus, all of the disciples would face some sort of persecution for living their faith
	- some of them were even crucified
	- had they known this, would they have still made the same choice?
	- so what might have helped them make the choice to leave behind their livelihood and their family and follow Jesus as a disciple?
	- to embrace the uncertainty for the future that would result from their choice?
- Jesus was preaching a message of "repent and believe in the good news"
	- Jesus's way and his preaching was radically different than anyone else in His time
	- He preached to forgive those things that are in the past and work to do better in future
	- So that might be part of what convinced the disciples to follow him
	- Jesus's healing acts may also have helped convinced them
	- Maybe the idea of serving others, helping make a difference in the lives of others and sharing their faith inspired them to make this choice
	- It's hard to say for sure

- But maybe in the presence of Jesus, they felt God's calling
	- Maybe their faith made them open to hearing from God the path that He wanted them to follow
	- This, I can understand as in 2013, I experienced a calling to become a Permanent Deacon.
	- I experienced a calling to help others and to share my faith through word and action
	- I can also understand the uncertainty that a decision like this might bring
	- There were lots of things I didn't know that I do now and there were a lot of questions that I had
	- But following this calling was something I chose to do
	- I felt like the right thing for me to do
	- So, maybe the disciples felt the same thing
	- My choice to become a Permanent Deacon has changed me, so that being a Permanent Deacon is just a part of who I am, something that I feel is a part of my identity, a part of my mission and purpose in life
	- so maybe this is why setting intentions instead of goals and resolutions resonated so much with me this year

- We all have our own plans and dreams for the future.
	- I know 15 years ago, becoming a Permanent Deacon was not one of my plans or dreams.
	- But God intended for me to become a Deacon and put that choice in front me.
	- And once I made that choice, to follow the path God put in front of me, he cleared many of the obstacles that would have prevented this from happening
	- now that I am a Deacon, I can't imagine not being a Deacon
	- Sometimes, we become fixed on our dreams and it becomes difficult to see another way forward when something is blocking our path.
	- My calling came at a time in my life when I was at one of these roadblocks in my life.  Not being sure what the way forward was.
	- But with faith, you can look at your choices and see if you can find God's hand at work in your life.
	- With faith, we can include God in our choices in order to discern the path forward, to become the person that God's intends us to be

- This 3rd Sunday of Ordinary Time is also designated as Bible Sunday in the Diocese of Hamilton.
	- Maybe you want to become someone who reads Scripture regularly.
	- This might be a good intention to work on this year
	- Daily or weekly reading of Scripture and journaling on the thoughts that come to mind might help you with this intention
	- Or even, reading the Sunday readings earlier in the week and letting them percolate in your thoughts through the week so that the Word of God can help you through the challenges of your week
	- These are choices we might consider to help us forward on our journey to be the person we want to be, the person God wants us to be.

- So, as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us consider:
	- the choices we make in our life, are they guided by our intention of who we want to be, and who God intends us to be?
	- know that with our choices, there will be uncertainty, but have faith that God has a plan for us
	- a plan to help us become the person we are meant to be
