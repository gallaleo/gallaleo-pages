# Gospel  
### Mark 4.26-34 of Sunday June 16, 2024

Such a large crowd gathered around Jesus that he got into a boat and began to teach them using many parables.

Jesus said: “The kingdom of God is as if a man would scatter seed on the ground, and would sleep and rise night and day, and the seed would sprout and grow, without his knowing how. The earth produces of itself, first the stalk, then the head, then the full grain in the head. But when the grain is ripe, at once he goes in with the sickle, because the harvest has come.”

Jesus also said, “With what can we compare the kingdom of God, or what parable will we use for it? It is like a mustard seed, which, when sown upon the ground, is the smallest of all the seeds on earth; yet when it is sown it grows up and becomes the greatest of all shrubs, and puts forth large branches, so that the birds of the air can make nests in its shade.”

With many such parables Jesus spoke the word to them, as they were able to hear it; he did not speak to them except in parables, but he explained everything in private to his disciples.

# Homily

- It is sometimes in our greatest need that we find the humility needed to let Jesus enter deeper into our hearts!
	- I know from my own experience that it is not easy to ask for help
	- I sometimes wonder why that might be
	- Sometimes, it means we have to admit that we don't have all the answers
	- that we need input from someone else and we might fear what others will think of us when we ask for help
	- Other times, asking for help might feel like we have failed, that we couldn't get the job done ourselves
	- One thing I have learned though, especially with the current team I work with in my job is that asking for help is also an opportunity to learn
		- An opportunity for me to learn from the person I have asked for help from, and also an opportunity for me to learn that asking for help allows someone else to share their gifts and knowledge, often for the greater good of the team
	- Asking for help though does mean admitting that we are in need and that takes humility
	- And God is always there for us if we reach out to Him

- In today's Gospel, Jesus shares two parables with the people assembled
	- the first is the parable of the sower
	- the second is the parable of the Mustard seed
	- the fruits of both of these parables refer to the Kingdom of God
	- Jesus uses parables because they allowed the people of the time and even of our time to see the presence of God's love for us in our everyday joys and challenges
	- God is truly present to us and interested in our lives and has given us many gifts to help us on our journey
	- I have found that the biggest lessons I have learned in life are through the lived experience of my day to day joys and challenges
	- so to me, this makes sense as a great way to teach people

- As a people of faith, we understand that a part of our mission is to help others and to help grow the Kingdom of God
	- and there are many different ways that we can do that
	- prayer leads us to a deeper friendship with God in our lives
	- sharing our faith through our deeds and our words is another way
	- patience, kindness, charity are some of the gifts that help us do that
	- often though, if we don't see results right away, we may get discouraged and lose faith
	- and that is what Jesus is trying to tell us in the first parable
	- the sower scatters the seeds everywhere
	- he doesn't worry about how the seeds will grow, he trusts in God that they will
	- when the time is right, the seeds will grow into something much bigger
	- God asks us to love thy neighbor, to help others, not for our own gain, but in service of the other
	- when we are kind to others, for no reason other than it is a good thing to do
		- sometimes we will be thanked and appreciated
		- and there will some times when we will not be thanked
		- but the point is that God will put our kind acts to His use
		- and we may not even see the impact our kindness has had on others 
			- and that's ok
		- but when the conditions are right, our kindness can be the very thing that gives a person hope in their future, 
			- the very thing that might restore their faith that they can move forward in their lives

- Sometimes, we may be dealing with a heavy burden in our lives
	- it may be that our workload is more than we think we can handle
	- we may feel that our lives are stuck in a hopeless situation
	- we may be dealing with the loss of a loved one
	- sometimes the situation we find ourselves in is of our own making
	- other times, we have had done nothing to bring the situation on ourselves
	- regardless, it is often at these moments of greatest need that we humbly find ourselves reaching out to Jesus for help

	- there may be times when we reach  out to Him, that it feels like He has abandoned us
	- but I've said this before and bears repeating
		- if we keep Jesus close to us through the joyful and normal times of our lives, it feels much easier to find Him when we are faced with a challenge and don't know where else to turn

 - that Jesus uses seeds in both of these parables brings up some interesting thoughts
	 - First, seeds are small and, because they are small, may seem insignificant
	 - But farmers and gardeners truly appreciate their value
	 - In the parable of the Sower, Jesus pictured God as the sower who scattered seeds on the ground
	 - And that is precisely how God works – always from humble and small beginnings: 
		 - from the manger in Bethlehem to the carpenter’s shop in Nazareth and to the cross in Calvary
		 - from twelve disciples in Galilee, each with their own faults to spreading the Word of God through the great forums of Rome and throughout the whole world

 - this teaches us a lesson on humility and littleness
	 - as a part of our human nature, we tend to seek for recognition and appreciation
	 - hence, people aspire to become big – rich, famous, and powerful
	 - but wisdom tells us that this is not the way to true greatness and joy
	 - those who seek riches and notoriety and power tend to keep looking for more once they find it
	 - or have to work so hard to keep what they have acquired 
	 - the seeds in the parables are a reminder that being little does not mean being powerless or insignificant
	 - Jesus is a perfect example of this
		 - He came into our world as an infant, being raised by His parents
		 - He became a humble but great preacher and worker of miracles
		 - and He sacrificed himself on the Cross for our salvation
		 - giving all of us the hope for eternal life
  
	- humility is also a trait we see in many of the great Saints like John the Baptist and St. Joseph
		 - St. Joseph was the protector of Jesus and helped raise Him to adulthood preparing Him to go out on His own to fulfil God's plan for Him
		 - an important job, raising the Son of God!!
		 - but have you ever noticed that in any Christmas cards of the Nativity scene how Joseph is always shown towards to the back of the scene, or even off to the side a bit?
	 - As Ralph Waldo Emerson said, “A great person is always willing to be little.” 
		 - In other words, it takes a lot of greatness to become little. The way of God is humility.

- so, as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us think about
	- how like it takes time for a seed to grow into the plant it is to become, 
		- it can take time for our kind acts and the things we do for others to bear fruit
	- how like the seeds start small and become great, 
		- with humility, we too can start small with the hope to become great in the Kingdom of God
	- how it is much easier to reach out to God for help when we keep Him involved in our lives on a daily basis
