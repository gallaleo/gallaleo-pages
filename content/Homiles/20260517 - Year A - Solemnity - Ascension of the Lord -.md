# First reading
## Acts 1.1-11 of Sunday May 17, 2026

In the first book, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus did and taught from the beginning until the day when he was taken up to heaven, after giving instructions through the Holy Spirit to the Apostles whom he had chosen. After his suffering he presented himself alive to them by many convincing proofs, appearing to them during forty days and speaking about the kingdom of God.  
  
While staying with them, he ordered them not to leave Jerusalem, but to wait there for the promise of the Father. “This,” he said, “is what you have heard from me; for John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now.”  
  
So when they had come together, they asked him, “Lord, is this the time when you will restore the kingdom to Israel?” He replied, “It is not for you to know the times or periods that the Father has set by his own authority. But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”  
  
When he had said this, as they were watching, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight. While he was going and they were gazing up toward heaven, suddenly two men in white robes stood by them. They said, “Men of Galilee, why do you stand looking up toward heaven? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go into heaven.”

# Homily

- A couple of thoughts came to mind as I was reflecting on the readings for today, but the one the floated to the top of my mind was the theme of 'transition'
	- this word, common used, means the process of changing from one state or condition to another
	- the term is used in many different fields
		- in the arts like painting, sculpture or writing, an idea is transitioned into a work of art that people can see or read and that may inspire them
		- in physics, an example of this is ice transitions from being a solid to a liquid when it melts
		- or water transitions from a liquid to a gas when it is boiled
		- steel is made by melting solid ores to a liquid and them cooling them into a solid state in the shape that is needed
			- well, it's more complicated than that, but you get the idea

- We go through many transitions in our lives
	- when we reflect on our lives to date, we can easily see transitions that we have experienced, especially the big transitions
	- some big transitions in my life include
		- going from being single to being married
		- moving from Prince Edward Island to Ontario
		- becoming a parent
		- becoming a Deacon
		- and the different jobs I've transitioned between in my career
	- and this is a poignant reminder that nothing in our earthly existence is permanent except for change
	- there is always something changing in our lives
		- from small day to day changes 
			- like the price of gas or the weather on any given day or hour
		- to impactful life changing events
			- like finding the love of your life, the birth of a child, the loss of a loved one
		- some changes are under our control, choices we make
		- other changes are beyond our control, changes we can't avoid, possibly due to choices made by other people
	- so, as I said, there is always something changing in our lives

- In today's readings, we hear about a transition that Jesus makes
	- we hear 'as they were watching, he was lifted up, and a cloud took him out of their sight'
	- when Jesus ascends to heaven, he transitions from an earthly presence, bound by the laws of time, present in our earthly existence, in human form
		- to a heavenly presence, seated at the right hand of the Father
	- He transitioned from a visible presence, a presence that could interact with the disciples, be seen by them, be touched by them, break bread with them
		- to an invisible presence, a spiritual presence, a sacramental presence
		- an invisible presence that is with us through the Holy Spirit
			- through our faith, through following Jesus' commandments

- In our liturgical life, the Ascension marks a transition between the Resurrection of Christ at Easter and the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost
	- we hear in the First Reading that 'He ordered them not to leave Jerusalem, but to wait there for the promise of the Father'
	- that promise that they would be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now
	- the apostles mission, to baptize in the name of the Father, and the Son and the Holy Spirit has been carried out through the generations to our present day
	- when we are baptized, we are baptized with water and the Holy Spirit, transitioning us to adopted children of God
	- changing us through the power of the Holy Spirit

- Through our baptism, we are asked to carry on that  same mission that the Apostles were commissioned to do
	- the nature of that mission, how we carry out that mission, may change as we go through transitions in our lives
	- but nature of our mission, to bring Jesus to others in our lives, through our actions, through the help of Holy Spirit is still the same
	- and although there will always be changes in our lives, one constant that is promised us is what Jesus said to the apostles:
		- 'And remember, I am with you always, to the end of the age'
	- Jesus is always with us
		- and it is part of our mission to open our hearts to Him to allow Him to participate in our lives
		- and this allows us to share our gifts with others, bringing Jesus to them

- Some of the transitions and changes we go through in our lives will be challenging, especially those we have not chosen for ourselves, those that are beyond our control
	- yet, even when the change is not within our control, we still have the control to choose how we respond
	- here is where leaning on the Holy Spirit can help us
	- help us in how we respond to those changes beyond our control
	- give us the hope we need to be able to move forward
		- to choose how we respond to the changes in our lives, to help us to trust in God

- Hope is powerful
	- having a sense of Hope gives us extra strength
	- our hope, rooted in our faith, gives us the strength we need to persevere in the face of challenge
	- many times in life, we are faced with challenges, some so large that they may cause us to feel helpless or overwhelmed
	- especially when we have no control or way to influence the challenges we are facing
	- but if we can find hope, that hope can give us the courage, strength and resilience to ride out the inevitable storms that life brings
	- Hope can help us transition through the challenge, moving forward with the wisdom that the challenge teaches us

- Something I have found that is essential to hope is connection to others  
	- hope is not solitary
	- as children of God, we are all deeply interconnected to those around us, many times in more ways than we realize
	- I have found that my hope is usually inspired through my connections with those people around me
	- and if you feel like you are losing hope, that impact can be felt by those around us
	- Hope is contagious though
	- since we are so interconnected, our hope also spreads to those around us
	- many times, when I am faced with a challenge that has me feeling overwhelmed or helpless, it is through my connections to others that I find hope that I need to work my way through the challenge
		- and I have to think that Jesus' invisible presence is working through those connections who have helped me

- Some of those connections may be casual or brief, maybe only with us through a single transition or challenge
	- where as others endure through the good times and the challenging times, accompanying us through many transitions
	- all of those connections become a part of our journey through life, making us who we are today
	- it is through our connections to others and through the help of the Holy Spirit that we can fulfil our mission, to go forth and make disciples of all the nations

- So, as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us consider:
	- the transitions that we have encountered in our lives
	- the sources of Hope that we have experienced in our lives
	- how we can choose to move through the transitions in our lives
		- in a way that can help us further the mission of Jesus
