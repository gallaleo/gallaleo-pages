## 20250216 - [[Year C - Ordinary Time - 6th Sunday]]

- When we are truly listening, we come to know that God is speaking to us, pointing the way, showing the direction forward. 
	- We simply need to learn to keep our ears open.

- In today's Gospel, we hear Jesus talk about Blessings and Woes
	- Blessed are you who are poor
	- Blessed are you who are hungry now
	- Blessed are you who weep now
	- Blessed are you when people hate you
	- Woe to you who are rich
	- Woe to you who are full now
	- Woe to you who are laughing now
	- Woe to you when all speak well of you

- When we look at how success is measured in society in our day and age, Jesus's words seem to be backwards.
	- the things we seem to strive for are to be rich, to be full, to be happy, to be respected by others
	- yet, Jesus says blessed are the poor, the hungry, the weeping, those who are hated
	- could this maybe mean that things have changed since Jesus's time?
	- I don't think so

- The words of Jesus are meant to be just as effective and impactful for our lives today as they were for the disciples and followers of Jesus in His time
- They are not meant as literature from the distant past to which we listen to, purely for their historical value. 
	- Jesus's words are just as relevant today as they were back in Jesus's time here on earth
	- The Word of God is living and active, meant to touch the very depths of our being
	- where we find our need for God, since none of us lives nor dies for self alone
	- In other words, the teaching of Jesus needs to be adapted to our setting and our particular needs
	- we all are in need of the same things as Jesus’ first hearers: 
		- the need to love and be loved without reserve
	- and no one is going to love us more than God does

- As difficult as it may be to admit, we all need God in our lives
	- If you think about the poor, the hungry, the mourning, those who are persecuted
	- and then think about the rich, the full, the happy, those who are well spoken off
	- who is more likely to admit that they have a need for God in their lives?
	- when we are not in need, we are less likely to admit that we need God's help'

- If you could erase all the mistakes of your past, you would also erase all of the wisdom of your present
	- These are not my words, but when I read them, they really stuck with me.
	- I have had people tell me that they have appreciated my wisdom, that I had the right words that they needed to hear to help them through something they were dealing with
	- I've also made my share of mistakes
	- Not sure if that makes me wise, but I do try to learn from my mistakes
	- And to be honest, although I try not to, I don't think I am done making mistakes
	- So, I guess it's fair to say that I am not done learning
	- And despite the challenges I face in my life, I always work to do my best to keep moving forward, even if it's not easy

- At the start of this Gospel, we hear something else important that stood out for me
	- we hear how Jesus came down with the twelve and stood on a level place, with a great crowd of His disciples and a great multitude of people around Him
	- Jesus, God that He was, came down and met us where we are
	- He humbled Himself to be among us, on the same level as us
	- He came and delivered His Word to us in person
	- And after He ascended into heaven, He left His wisdom to guide us and to inspire us and teach us how to discern the way forward

- He also left us the Eucharist, a remembrance of His sacrifice for our sake
	- so that even in our present times, Through the Eucharist, He comes down to our level
	- Jesus shares his divine nature with us, helping us to reconcile it with our human experience.
	- To show us His love for us, to strengthen our faith, to give us hope for our own salvation
	- That we may rejoice in that day and leap for joy when we receive our reward in heaven

- Blessed are you who are poor
	- this is reminder that the things many seem to value in this world are temporary
	- wealth, power, they do not last
	- we don't take them with us to the next life
	- it is a reminder to us that even those who are materially poor in our world, they are also children of God and as such are just as important as each one of us, deserving of respect and consideration
	- and no matter our material wealth in this world
	- or even our spiritual wealth
		- it is a reminder to us to cultivate a spirit of humility and to acknowledge our need for God in our lives
		- all we have comes from God's providence
	- it can be a challenge to make sure we don't become too comfortable in our lives as our material fortunes can easily change in a moment

- Blessed are you who are poor
	- this is a statement that challenges our assumptions about blessing and success.
		- it offers hope to the oppressed and those who struggle
		- it calls us to humility
		- and invites us to prioritize the kingdom of God in our lives
	- And as it says in the Psalm today
		- Blessed is the one who trusts in the Lord

- So as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us remember that
	- Blessedness is found not in what we have, but in our relationship with God
	- and just like some of our best friendships can challenge us to change, our relationship with God can also challenge us to change
	- and this will change the way we see ourselves, the way we see others, and the way we see the world
	- Remember, our faith and the practice of our faith grows our relationship with God
