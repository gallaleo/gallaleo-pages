# First Reading
## Isaiah 55.6-9 of Sunday September 20, 2026

Seek the Lord while he may be found,  
call upon him while he is near;  
let the wicked person forsake their way,  
and the unrighteous person their thoughts;  
let that person return to the Lord that he may have mercy on them,  
and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.  
  
For my thoughts are not your thoughts,  
nor are your ways my ways, says the Lord.  
For as the heavens are higher than the earth,  
so are my ways higher than your ways  
and my thoughts than your thoughts.

# Gospel
## Matthew 20.1-16 of Sunday September 20, 2026

Jesus spoke this parable to his disciples: The kingdom of heaven is like a landowner who went out early in the morning to hire labourers for his vineyard. After agreeing with the labourers for the usual daily wage, he sent them into his vineyard. When he went out about nine oclock, he saw others standing idle in the marketplace; and he said to them, You also go into the vineyard, and I will pay you whatever is right. So they went.  
  
When he went out again about noon and about three oclock, he did the same. And about five o'clock he went out and found others standing around; and he said to them, Why are you standing here idle all day? They said to him, Because no one has hired us. He said to them, You also go into the vineyard.  
  
When evening came, the owner of the vineyard said to his manager, Call the labourers and give them their pay, beginning with the last and then going to the first. When those hired about five oclock came, each of them received the usual daily wage.  
  
Now when the first came, they thought they would receive more; but each of them also received the usual daily wage. And when they received it, they grumbled against the landowner, saying, These last worked only one hour, and you have made them equal to us who have borne the burden of the day and the scorching heat. But he replied to one of them, Friend, I am doing you no wrong; did you not agree with me for the usual daily wage? Take what belongs to you and go; I choose to give to this last the same as I give to you. Am I not allowed to do what I choose with what belongs to me? Or are you envious because I am generous?  
  
So the last will be first, and the first will be last.


# Homily

- It's a hot summer day and you think, an ice cream sundae would go really good right now!
	- so, you get in the car and go to your favorite ice cream spot
	- you notice that a bunch of other people, like around 15 other people had the same idea as you, so there is bit of a line up ahead of you
	- but you want the ice cream, so you decide to get into the line and wait
	- the line up is moving slowly, and it is hot out in the sun
	- eventually, you get to the front of the line and place your order, maybe caramel fudge, or double chocolate
	- then a few minutes later, your order is ready and you go and sit down to enjoy your ice cream sundae
	- and it tastes wonderful!  and you feel it was worth the wait!

- as you are sitting down on a bench finishing your ice cream, you take a look at the line up now, and notice that the line up only has a couple of people in it
	- so the people who are in line up now had a way shorter wait for their tasty treat than you did!
	- Hmmm!   
	- The thought I will leave you with here is:
		- does the length of the waiting, the effort you put in to get your tasty treat, does it change how much you enjoyed it when you see others who don't have to wait so long?
		- Was our expectation for a short wait, or the reward of a tasty treat?
		- Does this diminish the reward you get from your treat?

- In today's Gospel, we hear a parable that Jesus tells, the one about the workers in the vineyard
	- In this parable, it starts with the workers who were hired early in the morning
	- They worked hard all day and did a good days work
	- When they started the day, they were happy to do the work for the agreed upon wage
	- At least, that was until the end of the day when they saw that the other workers who were hired later in the day got the same wage as they agreed to
	- When they saw this, their expectations changed
	- and then they were disappointed when they didn't get more when their turn to get paid came
	- this reminds me of that expression:
		- comparison is the thief of joy
		- had they not known what the others were paid, they probably would have been happy with what they were paid

- Let me offer another perspective on this parable
	- In the first reading we hear from Isaiah that the Lord's thoughts are not our thoughts,  nor are our ways His ways
	- We live in a world that can be very transaction based
	- sometimes, it may seem that we do something for someone else either because they've done something for us and we feel we owe them or we do something in the hopes of receiving something in return
	- and secondly, especially with how integrated social media is into our daily life, comparison to others is an all too easy trap to fall into
	- God's ways are different than our ways and as we see in the parable, God does not compare us to each other
	- He recognizes that we each have our own gifts and talents, those things that make each of us unique and special
	- He looks at each of us with an eye to our unique situation and our own needs and wants to guide us along the path that helps us become a better version of ourselves

- On the Cross at Calvary, Jesus gives the ultimate gift
	- He pays the full price for our salvation, giving redemption to anyone who accepts Him
	- He offers them mercy and forgiveness and eternal salvation
	- and this is regardless of whether we have been a life long follower of Jesus which parallels the workers hired first thing in the morning
	- or we have recently turned to Jesus in our lives, like the repentant thief who was crucified at Calvary with Jesus,  paralleling the workers hired later in the day by the land owner
	- this I think should change our expectations of entitlement and let us be more focused on our own efforts rather than comparing ourselves to others
	- and when we do see the accomplishments of others, should we not be happy for them

- And then there is the owner of the vineyard
	- the owner represents God the Father, who is generous with what He gives
	- He gives each of the workers a just wage
	- Just like He generously gives each of us many gifts and talents along with His unconditional love
	- And maybe some of us have to work harder than others to grow and use those gifts and talents
		- but often, it is through the challenges that we face that help us grow
		- I often find that I learn more from the mistakes I have made than from the situations where everything went smoothly
		- And I do make my share of mistakes!
		- Just ask my wife!!

- Everyone's journey is different and in comparing ourselves to others, we can sometimes forget that
	- we each have our own journey to undertake, our own path to follow
	- and I like to think that the path we follow, mistakes and all, helps us to learn the things we need to in our lives to help us find our way to God

- so, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist, let's think about this:
	- how long we stood in line for the ice cream treat that we wanted doesn't change how much we enjoy that treat
	- And maybe that's the point.
	- Jesus's sacrifice on the Cross teaches us that the true reward isn't a wage to be expected from our work, but a gift to be received from doing God's work through our actions
