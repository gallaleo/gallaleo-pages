# First reading
## Wisdom 3.1-9 of Sunday November 2, 2025

The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God,  
and no torment will ever touch them.  
In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to have died,  
and their departure was thought to be a disaster,  
and their going from us to be their destruction;  
but they are at peace.  
 
For though in the sight of others they were punished,  
their hope is full of immortality.  
Having been disciplined a little,  
they will receive great good,  
because God tested them  
and found them worthy of himself;  
like gold in the furnace he tried them,  
and like a sacrificial burnt offering he accepted them.  
  
In the time of their visitation they will shine forth,  
and will run like sparks through the stubble.  
They will govern nations and rule over peoples,  
and the Lord will reign over them forever.  
Those who trust in him will understand truth,  
and the faithful will abide with him in love,  
because grace and mercy are upon his holy ones,  
and he watches over his elect.

# Gospel
## Luke 7.11-17 of Sunday November 2, 2025

Jesus went to a town called Nain, and his disciples and a large crowd went with him. As he approached the gate of the town, a man who had died was being carried out. He was his mother’s only son, and she was a widow; and with her was a large crowd from the town. When the Lord saw her, he had compassion for her and said to her, “Do not weep.”

# Homily
- Begin with the end!
	- "Begin with the end" is a powerful call to live intentionally by first defining your success looks like before starting something
	- Anyone who works in a discipline where you create something can probably understand this concept
		- Like an artist who paints or sculpts, they often have a vision of what they are trying to create or what the end looks like before they start and that helps to guide them through the process of creating the finished work
		- Someone who is building something often has a blueprint or idea of what they are building so that they can build a foundation or structure that will support what they are trying to build
		- I think you get the idea!
	- Beginning with the end, this has been a theme I have thinking about the last couple of weeks and I think this theme can be applied to our lives and our plans for the future
	- I also think though that the journey from the beginning to the end is where the real learning happens
	- As we celebrate All Souls Day, as we commemorate the lives of the faithful departed, it is also an opportunity to think about our own lives and how we want to move forward

- Today's scripture readings help us to reflect on life, death, faith and hope
- In the Gospel, we hear about a widowed mother mourning over the body of her son
	- grief is how you FEEL on the inside after any loss
	- mourning is how we express those emotions of grief
	- Jesus has compassion for the widow and told her not to weep
	- Compassion is defined as the feeling of concern and sympathy for the suffering and misfortune of others, that also involves an active desire to help relieve that suffering
	- the root of the word is from Latin and mean "to suffer together"
	- Jesus shares in the suffering of the widow

- All Souls Day is a  celebration where we commemorate all of the faithful departed
	- Be they family or friends, or people we may have known even briefly, but have had an impact on our lives
	- Thinking about those we have lost can bring up feelings of sadness and pain
	- Grief for someone we have lost is perfectly normal
	- Our grief reflects the love that we have for them
	- expressing that grief through mourning is also a part of the grief process
	- And grief isn't something that goes away, rather it is something that becomes a part of us, just like the love of the person we lost is a part of us
	- But thinking about them is also a chance to reminisce about the good memories they bring to mind 
	- Those memories can bring back to us the joy of the time that we shared with them
	- When I think about my Grandfather, playing cards with him, listening to him play one of his instruments, or him being a Chevy man grudgingly admitting that my Ford Mustang is a nice car always brings a smile to my face

- As a society, we seem to struggle with how to approach and support people through their grief
	- It often feels like an isolating experience that people are expected to manage privately and quickly
	- Jesus did not look away from people as they were suffering though
	- He accompanied them in their struggles and challenges
	- He had compassion for them and looked at how He could help them
	- and in today's example, Jesus restores the widow's son to her, turning her tears of grief into tears of joy
	- Now we can't do the same miracles as Jesus did, but we can support those we know through their struggles and challenges, with compassion and patience
	- As a Deacon, my ministry of service is supporting and working with people who are experiencing grief through the When Life Changes program which I will be bringing to our Parish here in the new year

- Jesus brings life to those He touches
	- Jesus, the incarnation, is a God who listens, who shows mercy, who protects and saves
	- and although some day, we all must face death, we have hope that we will meet with Jesus in the eternal hereafter
	- Jesus conquered death, through His Crucifixion, and restored eternal life through His Resurrection
	- giving us hope for our own salvation
	- Jesus is the Lord of life!

- As I said, we need to have compassion to help those who are grieving
	- compassion needs us to also be patient
	- patience is the ability to accept or tolerate delay, trouble or suffering without becoming annoyed, anxious or upset
	- we often think about a lack of patience when we are stuck in a lineup or in traffic
	- but patience is needed when helping someone through a struggle or challenge
	- being patient requires us to give of our time, to act with love and kindness instead of reacting with frustration
	- it requires us to be fully present, setting aside our own agenda in order to help or listen to another's situation

- patience is one of those things that we often pray to God for more of
	- but let's think about that
		- when you pray for more patience, do you think God is just going to zap you and instantly you have more patience?
		- or is it more likely that the challenge you are facing, maybe is an opportunity that God put in front of you in order for you to practice your patience!

- In the first reading from the Book of Wisdom, we hear of God the creator
	- God gives us the gift of life, a life that does not end with death
	- Our relationship with God continues in the afterlife
	- like gold in a furnace, we will be tried
	- like a sacrificial burnt offering, we will be accepted

- according to a survey of hospice nurses that was done, some of the most common regrets of those who are dying were
	- wishing they had spent more time with the people they loved
	- wishing they had made spirituality more of a priority
	- learned to express their feelings more
	- realizing that happiness is a choice

- The hope of immortality, eternal life, provides a way of looking at life
	- the first reading promotes a just way of life, of justice, of righteousness, of doing the right thing
	- and if we look at our own life, there are days and weeks when it seems like a struggle just to get through it
	- but if we want to live an intentional life, now is the time to look forward and how we might do that
	- a life focused on the values of our faith
	- a life of making daily choices that reflect the hope for eternal life
	- a life lived beginning with the end in mind

- so as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist
	- On this All Souls Day, let us remember those we know who have died
	- let us have compassion and patience for those who are struggling with a loss
	- let us think about our life and how we live it, beginning with the end
