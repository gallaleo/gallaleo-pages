## 20250921 - [[Year C - Ordinary Time - 25th Sunday]]

- As I was reflecting on today's Gospel, it came to me, that as usual, there is a lot that one can take away from Jesus's words today
	- Jesus, in this case, was using this parable to make a point to the disciples, 
		- but these same words have been contemplated by and reflected on by many people over the years, 
		- with many of them coming up with different meanings that were relevant to their specific life situations
	- and so is often the case with the Word of God
	- the Word of God is dynamic, it is living and active, with the power to change peoples lives
		- for the people of our time, and for people throughout history

- One of the most obvious messages of the Gospel is that you cannot server two masters
	- serving God vs. serving wealth
	- in thinking about serving wealth, this really gets to the point of the importance in our lives of material possessions
	- and we live in a time that is flooded with advertisements and consumerism
	- where, how people regard us, is sometimes measured by our possessions, whether we have the latest popular phone or clothes 
	- and it is next to impossible to use the internet or social media without seeing advertisements trying to influence us in some way
		- and did you know that in the US, there is a refrigerator that  will now have advertisements shown on its display screen as part of a pilot program?
		- I don't know about you, but I don't need a screen on my fridge!!

- the message we get from the Word of God can have a lot to do with our life situation
	- we can receive a different message from a given scripture passage at different points in our life
- the message that really stands out for me from today's Gospel is the choices we make
	- the manager in the parable was accused of squandering the rich man's property
		- it doesn't say why he was doing this, but later in the parable, it says that the rich man noted that the manager had acted shrewdly in trying to find a new future for himself
		- so to me, this implies that the manager had been squandering the rich man's property by choice, looking out for his own interests, rather than fulfilling the duties of his work diligently
		- so the manager chose the path he was following

- when the rich man confronted him with what had been reported to him, the manager knew that his choices for his future where dim
	- while he still had the chance, he looked at how he might buy favor with the rich man's debtors, 
	- making concessions on what they owed, so that they would pay off their debts sooner
	- buying favor with the people who owed debts, but also resulting in the payment of their debts owed to the rich man sooner,
	- and the rich man commended the manager on his shrewdness

- in this case, the manager was able to choose to do something the change his situation
	- that is not always the case
	- but if you want your life to be different, you have to choose to make it happen

- in our lives, we all make many choices in the course of a day
	- we choose what is important to us in our lives
	- from simple choices like what to have for breakfast
	- to in some cases, large choices like whether to look for a new job or to stay where you are
	- some of the choices we make will have little or no impact on our lives while others will change the whole path of our future

- back in 1997, I had made the choice to look for a new job
	- at the time, I ended up having two job offers that I had to choose from
	- one would have taken me from my home in Prince Edward Island to work in New Brunswick
	- the one I chose eventually took me from PEI to here in Ontario
		- and that one choice put me on a path that eventually brought me to here where I chose to become a Permanent Deacon
		- sometimes, it can be many years before you realize the impact of the choices you have made, the path that your choices have placed you on

- and sometimes, the seemingly insignificant choices that we make in our lives can turn out to be really important
	- back in 1995 on September 22, a Friday evening, there were a few colleagues from work that were planning to go out for a bit after work
	- I wasn't planning to go out that night, but at the last minute, I chose to go out with them
	- and that night, I ended up meeting the woman who eventually chose become my wife
	- I'm glad she chose to go out that night as well :-)

- making choices in our lives isn't always easy, especially with the more important choices
	- but I also firmly believe that God has a plan for each of us and that He will guide us in the choices we make along that path if we choose faith as our path

- not all of our choices are good ones
	- the manager in the Gospel made a bad choice when he decided to squander the rich man's property
	- but we can also choose to make amends
	- we can choose to fix our mistakes and work to make better decisions in the future

- At the end of the day, it is we who choose where we spend our time and that will tell us what is important in our life
	- being busy is a choice, worrying is a choice
	- not having time for the priorities that we value in our life is a choice
- faith is also a choice
	- God gave us the freedom to make our own choices in our lives
	- Jesus tells us to believe in the Gospel
	- at the end of the day though, that is a choice each of us must make
	- our relationship with God is a relationship of love
	- and we have to choose to love

- for me, prayer is key making choices, at least for the bigger ones
	- prayer comes in many forms, but for me, contemplation is the form of prayer I find most helpful when making choices
	- contemplation is a wordless, passive form of prayer where you simply rest in the presence of God, sitting in stillness, thinking about your choices
	- stillness is time spent untouched by noise or movement, holding everything in pause, as if time itself is listening
		- Stillness is where presence deepens and meaning settles
		- stillness in prayer can help us to find the presence of Jesus within us
		- a presence that can help guide us in our choices

- even Jesus had to make choices
	- and He relied on prayer to guide Him in His choices
	- one of His most difficult choices was to follow the path where He was to die on the Cross for our salvation
	- a choice made with love, for us and for the Father

- So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist
	- what choices in our past can we see the hand of God at work in our lives
	- how do we invite God into the choices that we make in our lives
	- let us think about which master are we choosing to serve in our lives
