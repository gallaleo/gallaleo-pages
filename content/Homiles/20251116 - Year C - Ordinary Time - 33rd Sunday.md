## 20251116 - [[Year C - Ordinary Time - 33rd Sunday]]

- In today's Gospel, Jesus tells the disciples that the Temple, the central building of the Jewish faith will be destroyed
    - The temple was a sacred place of worship for the Jewish people and a part of the national identity of the people of Israel in Jesus' time
    - It was the place where the people made their offerings and sacrifices to God
    - A place where they would feel God's presence among them
    - Because of the Temple, they felt protected and near to God
    - So hearing that the Temple would be destroyed would be shocking to the disciples and something that would shake them up
    - Maybe even cause them to wonder if God was abandoning them

- I was thinking about perspective, the particular way we think about and look at the things in our lives, and how it greatly impacts both our thoughts and our actions
- Some of the most common types of factors that might influence our perspective are personal factors, social factors and situational factors

- Personal factors are elements specific to each person
	- like the experiences they have had in their life
	- the beliefs and values that help us to determine what is right, important and meaningful to us
	- our skills and talents, the things they have a natural aptitude for or have invested time in learning
		- some people might be great at math, or music, or are great with people
		- these things might be easy for some of us whereas others might struggle with these things
	- our personality traits, like being an introvert vs extrovert, being optimistic vs pessimistic, for example
		- many years ago at work, everyone on the team I worked with took a personality test as a way of helping us to be able to improve how we worked as a team
		- then each of us was given a funny prayer that aligned with the personality category that we were grouped under
		- mine was:
			- Lord, help me to be more laid back
			- and help me to do it EXACTLY RIGHT!
		- those who know me well can understand how fitting that was

- Social factors come from the environment and the groups we belong to
	- our culture and our upbringing
	- our family and peer groups, the people we spend time with
	- our education and the information we consume through various forms of media
	- our financial status and our social class

- Situational factors are temporary or immediate factors that may affect us at a specific moment in time
	- like our current goals and needs
		- if you are hungry, you might view a restaurant sign differently than if you had just ate
	- our role and environment
		- time spent leading a group of people in a meeting at work gives us a different perspective than time being with a group of people at a social gathering
	- our emotional and physiological status
		- whether a person is stressed, happy or sick would affect that person's perspective

- So, when you look at all of these factors that impact our perspective on any given topic, we can quickly see that each person's perspective is a very complex and unique thing
	- suffice to say that we are all unique and have our own distinct perspective on any given topic, although in many cases, it may align with someone else's perspective

- Our perspective on any given topic will determine how we react
	- whether we feel confident, anxious, curious, defeated
	- the Jewish people, hearing that the Temple would be destroyed, would likely feel angry, confused, probably anxious

- Something interesting I read the other day said that the opposite of anxiety isn't calm, but rather it is trust!
	- and while calm may feel like the opposite of anxiety, I believe it is trust that brings about our transition from anxiety to calmness
	- the kind of trust that doesn't ask for proof
	- the kind of trust that shows up when nothing else makes sense
	- the kind of trust that you feel in your bones, because your brain hasn't figured it out yet
	- because doors get shut in our lives
	- jobs end
	- relationships fade away
	- Temples can get destroyed
	- and that was really one of the perspectives that Jesus wanted us to see
	- He wanted us to shift our view from an earthly perspective to an eternal perspective
	- Jesus's prophesied that "not one stone will be left upon another" referring to the Temple, but also meaning the things of this earth
	- the material things we spend all our time pursuing are not the things that will help us find salvation
	- it is not for these things that Jesus sacrificed Himself on the Cross for
	- He sacrificed Himself for our salvation
	- and it is our faith, our trust in Jesus, and the actions we perform inspired by that trust and faith that will help us on the path to salvation

- looking back at my own life, times in the past when I had felt anxious, unsure and confused about how the future would unfold before me
	- those times where it felt like things were falling apart
	- it was really God rearranging things for my own good
	- I didn't need to understand it all at the time
	- I just needed to trust in God, who does understand it
	- to have faith that God the best in mind for me
	- because trust doesn't come from being in control
	- it comes from being present, from choosing to accept that which you cannot change
	- from having faith in God
	- and sometimes the plans I made that didn't work out the way I thought, often enough, they turned out better than I could have planned for myself
	- that's just the way God works in our lives


- we have to continue to develop our spiritual life, where joy is found in embracing sorrow and happiness, pain and pleasure
	- A joy that is deeper, fuller, more
	- A joy that remains with us
	- A joy that is built on the trust we have in God and His plan for us
	- we have to persevere through the challenges we face to be able to see that joy
	- That joy that is something of God that is very profound
	- Something we can experience even when we are in the midst of challenge or persecution in our lives
	- in moments of sickness, in moments of health, 
	- in moments of success, in moments of failure, 
	- in moments of birth, in moments of death
	- Our perspective on our faith, our trust in God in our life can go a long way towards finding the joy of God, 
	- A joy that will carry us through the difficulties we face in our life
	- A joy that will keep us focused on our salvation

- so as we prepare the celebrate the Eucharist, let us think about:
	- our perspective on life and our focus on our salvation
	- how trust in God is the essence of our faith
	- how our faith, inspired by our trust in God can bring joy and the hope of salvation
