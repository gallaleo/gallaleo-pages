# Homily

- Do you ever feel like you are your own worst enemy? 
	- Have you ever thought you could accomplish great things if only you weren't so busy with so many little things? 
	- Are you tired of not getting the things done you want to?
	- If you've ever tried to accomplish anything worthwhile, then you've been face-to-face with resistance. 
	- Resistance loves keeping us busy with anything but the one thing that will most help us grow.
	- What is resistance? 
		- It's that sluggish feeling of not wanting to do something that you know is good for you.
		- It's the inclination to do something that you unabashedly know is not good for you, and it's everything in between. 
		- It's the desire and tendency to delay something you should be doing right now.
	- Sometimes, that's how I feel about repentance!!
	- I sometimes resist working on it.

- The last time I preached, I talked about Intentions and how I was using them to help me to become the person I wanted to be.  
	- To be someone who help others and to be a better person.
	- But despite having these intentions to guide me, sometimes I do things that I later regret.
	- I have to admit, that despite my best intentions, I am a sinner
	- And even though I know that I will feel better after I repent, I sometimes still feel resistant to repenting.
	- Most of the time, it's my own ego getting in the way.  
	- Not wanting to feel embarrassed or not wanting to admit that I have done something wrong.
	- And often for a lot of us, admitting that we are wrong is not easy.

- In today's Gospel, we hear that the Spirit drove Jesus out into the desert.
	- He was just baptized and is preparing to start His ministry here on Earth.  
	- He remained in the desert for forty days, to be tempted by Satan
	- In this Gospel, Mark doesn't go into the details of the temptations like the other Gospel writers do.
	- But we do know that Jesus resisted the temptations of Satan.
	- After the forty days, Jesus came to Galilee to proclaim the Gospel of God.
	- He preached that the kingdom of God is at hand!"
	- To repent and believe in the Gospel!

- We are all human and we are all sinners
	- We have all done things that we regret.
	- Things that have hurt our relationships with others and with God.
	- God recognizes this and reminds us of both of this and of His mercy as part of our Mass
	- To help put us into the proper state of mind for the celebration of the Word of God and of the Eucharist
		- we have the Penitential Act as a part of the Introductory Rite of the Mass
	- In the version of the Penitential Act we used today, we acknowledge that Jesus was sent to heal us, to heal the contrite of heart and we ask for God's mercy.
	- That Christ came for us, who are sinners
	- and that Jesus sits at the right hand of the Father, to intercede on our behalf for God's mercy.

- Contrition and repentance are two words often used interchangeably, but they have distinct meanings and implications. 
	- While both involve feelings of remorse and the desire to make amends, there are subtle differences between the two. 
	- Contrition refers to a deep sense of remorse or guilt for past actions, accompanied by a sincere desire to change and seek forgiveness. 
	- Repentance, on the other hand, goes beyond remorse and includes a turning away from the wrongdoings, leading to a change of behavior and a commitment to do better in the future.

- Our relationships with others play an important part in our lives.
	- And when things are broken in our relationships, it can throw us off and take us away from being the person we are meant to be.
	- Some times you have to accept your own brokenness before you can move forward!!
	- This acceptance may mean making a change, talking about it, or sometimes even just accepting the ways things are for now.
	- But choosing to accept it is the first step in the process of healing, the first step in the process of repentance.
	- Sometimes you need to sit and process the uncomfortable emotions associated with accepting that you made a mistake.
	- Sometimes you have to be ok with the fact that we are all sinners and that we all make mistakes.
	- Then we can look at going beyond contrition and moving towards making things right again.
	- While it's not easy, reconciling things with others and with God will make the difficult parts of the process worthwhile as I can affirm that you will usually feel better after making things right.

- In the 2nd Reading, we hear from St. Peter how Christ suffered for sins once for all.
	- for the righteous and the unrighteous in order to bring us to God
	- His death on the Cross, and his Resurrection gives us the hope that we can be saved
	- That even though we have sinned, there is a path to reconciliation and eventually for our own salvation.

- Our lives are busy, filled with many competing priorities.
	- Sometimes, we call these competing priorities "distractions" or temptations.
	- It is important to discern the difference between these temptations and things that are actual priorities in our lives.
	- How do you tell the difference between these temptations and the actual priorities in our life?
	- The activities that help us to be the person we are called to be, that help us move forward on our journey are from the Spirit
	- Those things that take us away from that path are temptations.

- The season of Lent is that reminder to step away from these temptations and take some time to look at who we are and the path we should be following.
	- We pray that in this time of salvation, that God will fill us with the Holy Spirit, purify our hearts and strengthen us in love
	- We pray that we will abstain from what we do not really need in our lives and help our brothers and sisters in distress
	- We pray that we will be loving not only in the great and exceptional moments in our lives, but also in the ordinary events of our day to day lives
	- We pray that we can live true to the calling of our Baptism, our calling as adopted sons and daughters of God

- So, as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us think:
	- how can we make the best of our Lenten season to become better versions of ourselves
	- to become the person God wants us to be
	- let us think about those things that are temptations for us, what we need to repent for and how we can make things right
	- how we can reconcile our ways with God's plan for us
