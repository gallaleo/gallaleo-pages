# Gospel

### Luke 9.28b-36 of Sunday March 16, 2025

Jesus took with him Peter and John and James, and went up on the mountain to pray. And while he was praying, the appearance of his face changed, and his clothes became dazzling white.  
  
Suddenly they saw two men, Moses and Elijah, talking to Jesus. They appeared in glory and were speaking of his exodus, which he was about to accomplish at Jerusalem.  
  
Now Peter and his companions were weighed down with sleep; but since they had stayed awake, they saw his glory and the two men who stood with him.  
  
Just as they were leaving him, Peter said to Jesus, “Master, it is good for us to be here; let us make three dwellings, one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah,” but Peter did not know what he said.  
  
While he was saying this, a cloud came and overshadowed them; and they were terrified as they entered the cloud. Then from the cloud came a voice that said, “This is my Son, my Chosen; listen to him!” When the voice had spoken, Jesus was found alone.  
  
And the disciples kept silent and in those days told no one any of the things they had seen.


# Homily


- A man asked his gardener how his plants grew so beautifully
- The gardener said:
	- I don't force them to grow, I remove what stops them from growing

- Temptation takes us away from things that help us to grow
	- the definition of temptation is the desire or impulse to do something, especially something wrong or unwise
	- I had been thinking about what temptation meant this week for a couple of reasons

- Firstly, we are in the season of Lent
	- and Lent is a time of seeking the Lord in prayer, 
		- serving God and others by giving alms and our time 
		- and of practicing self-control through fasting
	- These are all ways of helping to reconcile ourselves with the Lord
	- Fasting is a means of self-discipline, or restraining ourselves from our appetites, from those things we may find tempting

- Secondly, last week's Gospel from the First Sunday of Lent told of Jesus fasting in the desert for 40 days and 40 nights and of the devil's trying to tempt Jesus
	- And why might this have been chosen as the Gospel for the First Sunday of Lent?
		- I have to think that this is because we constantly live with temptation all around us and have since the time of Adam and Eve
		- they were forbidden to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil
		- but the serpent came and tempted them and they fell to temptation
		- When we pray the Lord's Prayer, we say "lead us not into temptation"
		- so temptation is something important enough in our lives to be included in one of the most used prayers in the Church

- Now, in today's Gospel, it starts with Jesus going up to the mountain to pray
	- Jesus took Peter, John and James with Him
	- Jesus often went up into the mountains by Himself when He needed to pray
	- I have to think that a part of this was to get away from everything, to find space to be with God without any distractions
	- I also think that it must help our prayer, being out in nature, in God's creation, when we are trying to connect with God

- On the high mountain with Jesus, the disciples Peter, John and James saw Jesus in His full glory
    - As Jesus prayed, His appearance changed and His clothes became dazzling white
    - Jesus was transfigured in his prayer, 
	    - His divinity was revealed, 
	    - He was shown in His full Glory
    - And then Jesus was talking to Moses and Elijah
    - But then a cloud came and overshadowed the disciples and from the cloud, God spoke to the them.
        - He said "This is my son, my Chosen; listen to him."
    - God spoke to the disciples and told them to "listen to Jesus"
    - To me, this is probably one of the most important messages from todays Gospel reading
    - "Listen to Jesus"

- The disciples had Jesus there in front of them to be able to listen to him.
    - But this got me thinking about how do I listen to Jesus in my life?
    - The words of Jesus are written in the Gospels. They are full of instruction and direction for us within their texts
    - We can listen to Jesus by taking time to read and reflect on the Gospels and how they can guide us in our lives

- Prayer is another way that we can listen to Jesus in our lives
	- One can pray anywhere
	- but given the opportunity, praying in silence, without the normal distractions around us
		- I have always found this to be a most effective way to listen to Jesus
	- Maybe not always easier, but certainly most effective

- Spending time in silence seems to be such a rarity these days
	- there is always something that needs to be done
	- the To Do list seems to have an ever growing list of things that I want to do on it
	- but sitting in silence, 
		- giving yourself time and space to pray, away from distractions has many benefits

- It gives you time to clear the random thoughts out of your head
	- when you first try sitting in silence, there will likely be a myriad of thoughts flowing through your head
	- but part of praying in silence is learning to just let them go
	- this helps us to focus more on what is important in our life

- Sitting in silence gives us a chance to get to know ourselves better
	- to think about our journey, to find clarity in our lives
	- to see the things that have meaning in our life
	- to see and think about the problems in our lives and with Jesus, figure out how we might solve those problems

- With time and practice, as you learn to clear the noise from your head and your thoughts get clearer
	- you can start to connect with Jesus more easily in your prayer
	- The closer you become to Jesus, the easier the process will become
	- It's one of those things where you just have to Trust the Process

- During this season of Lent
	- we want our focus to be on preparing ourselves to celebrate Easter, remembering the sacrifice that Jesus undertook for us, dying on the cross 
	- and the joy of His resurrection from the dead that gives us the hope for our own salvation
- To help us prepare
	- we want to focus more on prayer
	- we want to focus more on alms giving
	- we want to focus more on fasting
- but doing all those things requires self-discipline and practice
- and while temptation is defined as a desire for something wrong or unwise, self-discipline is a desire for improving ourselves

- Removing temptation from our lives is probably next to impossible
	- there will always be temptation in our lives
	- and as humans, we have lived with temptation since the time of Adam and Eve
	- but prayer is one of those things that we, as the gardeners of our lives, can use to resist temptation and help us to grow in our faith

- So, as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist,
	- let us think about the temptations in our lives
	- about how prayer can be the thing that helps us to resist those temptations we face
	- how our connection with Jesus helps us to strengthen and grow in our faith as we continue our Lenten preparation
