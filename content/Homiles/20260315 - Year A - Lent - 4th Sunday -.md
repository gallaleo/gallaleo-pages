# Gospel

## John 9.1, 6-9, 13-17, 34-38 (shorter) of Sunday March 15, 2026

(For the longer version, see the print edition of Living with Christ.)  
  
As Jesus walked along, he saw a man blind from birth.  
  
He spat on the ground and made mud with the saliva and spread the mud on the man’s eyes, saying to him, “Go, wash in the pool of Siloam” (which means Sent).  
  
Then the man who was blind went and washed, and came back able to see. The neighbours and those who had seen him before as a beggar began to ask, “Is this not the man who used to sit and beg?” Some were saying, “It is he.” Others were saying, “No, but it is someone like him.” He kept saying, “I am the man.”  
  
They brought to the Pharisees the man who had formerly been blind. Now it was a Sabbath day when Jesus made the mud and opened his eyes. Then the Pharisees also began to ask him how he had received his sight. He said to them, “He put mud on my eyes. Then I washed, and now I see.” Some of the Pharisees said, “This man is not from God, for he does not observe the Sabbath.” But others said, “How can a man who is a sinner perform such signs?” And they were divided. So they said again to the blind man, “What do you say about him? It was your eyes he opened.” He said, “He is a Prophet.”  
  
They answered him, “You were born entirely in sins, and are you trying to teach us?” And they drove him out.  
  
Jesus heard that they had driven him out, and when he found him, he said, “Do you believe in the Son of Man?” He answered, “And who is he, sir? Tell me, so that I may believe in him.” Jesus said to him, “You have seen him, and the one speaking with you is he.” He said, “Lord, I believe.” And he worshipped him.


# Homily

- We often see things as we are, not as they are
	- When you change the way you look at things, the things you look at change!!
	- Our reactions to things in our lives have a lot to do with our perspective,  how we see things
	- To me, the ability to stop and look at things from different points of view is an important gift
	- When I can stop to look at things from different perspectives, this is something that opens my eyes and helps me on my faith journey

- When I first read today's Gospel as I was preparing for today, one thing that struck me was that John mentioned that the blind man was blind from birth
	- As opposed to someone who had sight when they were born and only lost their sight later in life.
	- I'm not sure which situation would be more difficult to live with
	- And one thing I have come to learn is that when a detail like that is included in Scripture, it's an important point
	- Something you need to take a closer look at
	- Something that makes me think Why?
	- And why is one of my favourite questions!

- So we know that words are important
	- In fact, if you look at the beginning of John's Gospel, Chapter 1, verses 1 to 5, it says the following:
		- In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God
		- He was in the beginning with God
		- All things came into being through him, and without him not one thing came into being
		- What has come into being in him was life, and the life was the light of all people
		- The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not overcome it
	- Jesus, the Son of God, is 'the Word' talked about in John's Gospel
	- Jesus has the power to create, to bring light to our darkness!

- We also hear in the Gospel that the blind man was a beggar
	- In Jesus's time, if you were blind, there wasn't many ways to support yourself other than begging
	- From the perspective of most people who knew the blind beggar, he had little value to society and was someone who has tolerated
	- Thankfully times have changed
	- As humanity learns more about how our bodies work and with the advances in medicine and technology, there are a lot more options available for someone who is blind.

- In today's society, we would typically call someone who is blind disabled.
	- But disabled is that word that I have never really liked.
	- I know the word disabled does serve a purpose in raising awareness for groups of people in our society who may lack an ability that most of us have.
	- But the word seems to bring the focus to the gifts and talents that they don't have rather then the gifts and talents that they do have.
	- From my perspective, I like to think that instead of considering someone disabled, that we consider the gift and talents they do have.
	- I too have my own set of gifts and talents that are unique to me.  
	- And some talents that I excel in, others may find difficult, and there are those skills that others excel at that I know are difficult for me.
	- God has given us each of our gifts and talents.  He sees the potential for what we can do with those gifts.
	- And when we take all our individual gifts and talents and work together in community, we can do amazing things.

- Even more important than the blind man being given the gift of physical sight though, was removing the man's spiritual blindness
	- When the Pharisees first questioned the man upon learning about him receiving his sight, they asked him who did this?
		- The man responded that it was a man called Jesus
	- When they questioned him again, asking him what he had to say about Jesus, the man responded that "He is a prophet"
	- The Pharisees see things from their perspective, not opening their eyes to other possibilities
	- When Jesus speaks to the man later, the man born blind comes to see Jesus as the Lord and professes his faith saying "Lord, I believe"
	- He sees Jesus from a different perspective
	- The man's faith and his belief in who Jesus was, grew between his initial encounter with Jesus and his subsequent encounter with Him
	- And so is the nature of our faith
	- It isn't something that you can check off on your to-do list
		- I have Faith!  Check.  Done.  Now what's next!

	- It doesn't work that way
	- Faith is a journey, a journey that we travel along through our whole life
		- There are twists and turns on the journey, peaks and valleys that we will traverse
		- There will be people and events that will open our eyes to different perspectives
		- There will be times when our eyes are opened and our blindness will be removed
		- Faith is a choice that we will need to choose again and again at those pivotal moments in our life
	- Pope Benedict said 'Faith is not maintained automatically. It is not a ‘finished business’ that we can simply take for granted. The life of faith has to be constantly renewed.'

- Another important point I took from today's Gospel is that faith requires action on our part
	- Jesus certainly had the power to snap his fingers and give the man sight
	- But that isn't how things went with the blind man
	- Jesus spat on the ground, made some mud and then put the mud on the man's eyes
	- He then had the man go to the pool of Siloam and wash the mud off
	- The man had to take action, he had to take an active part in his healing, in his faith journey
	- He had to choose to act, he had to choose healing, he had to choose faith

- If we want to grow in our faith, it doesn't just happen
	- It requires action on our part
	- Our faith will be challenged by the people and events that happen around us
	- We will have to choose how we are going to respond to those people and events
	- We have to challenge our perspectives.  Are we truly seeing things as they are
	- We have to act on our faith, through works of mercy and kindness and generosity

- So as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist,
	- Are we set in our ways?  Or do we look at things from different perspectives?
	- What actions are we taking to grow our faith?
	- Do we believe in the Lord like the blind man came to believe?
