# First reading
## Sirach 15.15-20 of Sunday February 15, 2026

If you choose, you can keep the commandments, and they will save you. If you trust in God, you too shall live, and to act faithfully is a matter of your own choice. 

The Lord has placed before you fire and water; stretch out your hand for whichever you choose. Before each person are life and death, good and evil and whichever one chooses, that shall be given.  

For great is the wisdom of the Lord; he is mighty in power and sees everything; his eyes are on those who fear him, and he knows every human action. He has not commanded anyone to be wicked, and he has not given anyone permission to sin.

# Gospel

## Matthew 5. 17, 20- 24, 27-28, 33-34, 37 (shorter) of Sunday February 15, 2026

(For the longer version, see the print edition of Living with Christ.)  

Jesus said to his disciples: “Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have come not to abolish but to fulfill.  

“For I tell you, unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.  

“You have heard that it was said to those of ancient times, ‘You shall not murder’; and ‘whoever murders shall be liable to judgment.’ But I say to you that the one who is angry with their brother or sister, will be liable to judgment; and whoever insults their brother or sister, will be liable to the council; and whoever says, ‘You fool,’ will be liable to the hell of fire.  

“So when you are offering your gift at the altar, if you remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift.  

“You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall not commit adultery.’ But I say to you that everyone who looks at a woman with lust has already committed adultery with her in his heart.  

“Again, you have heard that it was said to those of ancient times, ‘You shall not swear falsely, but carry out the vows you have made to the Lord.’ But I say to you: Do not swear at all.  

“Let your word be ‘Yes,’ if ‘Yes,’ or ‘No,’ if ‘No’; anything more than this comes from the evil one.”


# Homily

- A river reached the desert and stopped
	- ahead was dry land
	- it could not cross, it feared disappearing
	- the Wind whispered "Let yourself rise and fall as rain.  You won't be lost.  You'll become more."
	- the river resisted,  it knew how to flow, not vanish
	- but holding on meant staying stuck forever
	- so, it surrendered into the air and returned as rain... becoming a river again on the other side

- And the message here is that transformation never destroys you, it moves you forward
	- Sometimes the moment we feel most stuck is the moment life is asking us to do something differently.
	- Just like the river, we learn to travel further once we stop trying to control the current
	- Once we choose to allow ourselves to trust the path God has laid before us
	- Rather than resisting what’s changing in our lives that we have no control over, can we surrender to how life is unfolding before us?
	- Can we trust that the bigger picture is carrying us forward to where we need to be, not undoing us?
	- This is a choice we may need to make at different points in our lives
	- And it is through the choices that we make in our lives that enable us to transform

- At the end of the week when I look back and think about what happened in my week, I think about how things went, things that brought me joy, and one of the things I also think about are the choices that I have made
    - Not so much from a "what could have been" perspective. I am usually pretty pragmatic and try not to dwell on the past. What's done is done. You can't undo the past
    - No, I look at things thinking more about did I do the right thing with the choices I made
    - Because life is essentially a long string of choices, ranging from the mundane "Should I hit the snooze button?" to the life-altering "Should I move across the country?"
    - If we don't take time to reflect on our choices, especially the major ones, how can we get better at making choices?

- In the first reading, from Sirach, we hear about having to make choices in how to live our lives
    - The Book of Sirach is from the part of the Old Testament referred to as Wisdom Literature
    - It is meant to help guide us in our human struggles and real-life experiences
    - This reading tell us that before us are only two choices: fire or water, good or evil, death or life

- And making choices in our lives can certainly be a struggle
    - Some choices are easy
    - Like when you have to choose between a slice of apple pie or a slice of cherry pie for dessert
        - Easy. Just have a slice of each. Problem solved
    - But other choices in life are much more difficult, especially when they can greatly impact how our lives will unfold
    - Most researchers and psychologists estimate that the average adult makes about 35,000 remotely conscious decisions each day
    - While that sounds exhausting, your brain is a master of automation. A lot of choices that we make in the course of a day are choices we make automatically, out of habit
    - If you had to think deeply about every single one, you’d never make it out of bed

- This freedom of choice is a gift from God. Life might be a lot easier if we never had to make any decisions and everything was decided for us
    - But would we really be living?
    - God gives us the freedom to choose, with the knowledge that there are consequences to our decisions if we don't choose wisely
    - Like having two slices of pie for dessert every time may impact how our clothes fit us

 - While all choices that we make in the course of our day have the power to transform and change our lives, some choices will impact us more than others
	- That is why God gave us the 10 Commandments in the Old Testament to guide us
	- Yet, one of the challenges we as humans have had with the 10 Commandments is our tendency to focus on the letter of the law and neglect the spirit of the law
	- What does that mean?
	- That's where Jesus's words in today's Gospel help us

- Today's Gospel, gives us some wisdom that we can look at to help us in our decision making
    - Jesus speaks about the law and how he is not here to abolish it, but to bring it to fulfilment
    - God's law is there not to constrain us, but to guide us and to help us to live in harmony with God. To guide us to the promise of salvation

- Jesus doesn't introduce new laws to replace the 10 Commandments, but rather adds clarifications to the Commandments that God gave us
	- Jesus explains that judgement will fall not just on those who commit murder, but on those who are angry with their brother or sister
	- And He explains that judgement will fall not just on those who commit adultery, but on those who look at another with lust
	- What is important about these explanations is that anger and lust start destroying your relationships with others and with God
	- Jesus points out that our intent is important in how we follow the laws, that we just don't follow the laws blindly
	- He wants us to know why we should follow the law
	- And that by following the law and knowing why we follow the law, this takes us closer to loving God

- And know that one of the things about having the freedom to make choices is that we will also make mistakes
    - In some cases, that is how we learn wisdom, how we learn to make better choices in the future
    - And that is a part of God's plan, that we can choose to learn from our mistakes
    - As is forgiveness for helping to reconcile our relationships with God and with others

- Jesus says "So when you are offering your gift at the altar, if you remember that your brother or sister has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar and go; first be reconciled to your brother or sister, and then come and offer your gift."
	- We want to keep our relationship with God in good standing. We want to have peace in our lives
	- What Jesus tells us here is,  if we are not at peace with our neighbor, our relationship with God won't be fully at peace either until we restore peace with our neighbor
	- If you have a dispute with your neighbor, you need to seek forgiveness or give forgiveness, to work towards peace with your neighbor
	- To seek forgiveness or to give forgiveness is a choice that we need to make.
	- God doesn't make us take these actions
	- He gives us the freedom to choose

- And Jesus is no stranger to making choices either. He chose to align His will with that of God's
    - Jesus through prayer and reflection made the choice to follow God's plan for Him
    - To follow the path that led to Him being crucified on the Cross, dying and then rising from the dead
    - He chose this path, to make this sacrifice for our salvation

-  So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist, let us think about
    - taking some time to reflect on the choices you have made, the choices that in the end transform our lives
    - looking at  how we can choose to make peace with our neighbor and God
    - seeking God's help with the choices that will make a difference in our lives
