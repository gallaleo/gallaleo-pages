# Gospel
## Matthew 5:1–12a

When Jesus saw the crowds, he went up the mountain,
   and after he had sat down, his disciples came to him.
He began to teach them, saying:

   “Blessed are the poor in spirit,
      for theirs is the Kingdom of heaven.
   Blessed are they who mourn,
      for they will be comforted.
   Blessed are the meek,
      for they will inherit the land.
   Blessed are they who hunger and thirst for righteousness,
      for they will be satisfied.
   Blessed are the merciful,
      for they will be shown mercy.
   Blessed are the clean of heart,
      for they will see God.
   Blessed are the peacemakers,
      for they will be called children of God.
   Blessed are they who are persecuted for the sake of righteousness,
      for theirs is the Kingdom of heaven.
   Blessed are you when they insult you and persecute you
      and utter every kind of evil against you falsely because of me.
   Rejoice and be glad,
      for your reward will be great in heaven.”

# Homily

- today, we celebrate the Solemnity of All Saints
	- a day when we honor the Saints
		- those spiritual super heroes who have been Canonized that we look to for inspiration
	- and those ordinary saints
		- the ordinary, every day faithful who walked through their lives striving to live their faith
		- we celebrate them today too
	- we celebrate all those who have entered heaven to be united with our Lord and saviour Jesus Christ

- we are also reminded on this day that we too are all called to holiness
	- which may feel challenging or even out of reach sometimes in the chaotic and messy times we seem to be living in

- something I have learned from some of my reading about the lives of the Saints is that the Saints also had messy, flawed pasts
	- Sainthood isn't reserved for people born perfect.
		- St. Augustine led a notoriously wild youth
		- St. Ignatius had a massive ego before his conversion
		- St. Peter denied knowing Jesus three times before Jesus was Crucified
	- A saint isn't someone who never fell;
		- they are simply people who kept getting back up after a making a mistake and trying to do better, relying on God's grace to rebuild them
	- and that gives me hope that we can all become saints

- in today's Gospel, we hear Jesus teach the well known Beatitudes
	- the Beatitudes present a different way of looking at daily life
	- a way that in Jesus's time and still to this day was counter to the societies view of what success should look like
	- we live in a world that seems to value wealth, self-reliance, dominance, status, and avoiding suffering
	- Jesus tells us that Blessed are those whose hearts are disposed to poverty of spirit, mourning, meekness, mercy, purity of heart, peacemaking
	- this reminds us that God values the humble, the meek, the brokenhearted, and those who suffer for righteousness

- trying to master all of these Beatitudes may still seem like a daunting task
- our first thought may be "How can we be all those things?"
	- but, as we learn from the Saints, Holiness is built in ordinary, tiny choices that are made every day in our lives
	- you don't need to perform grand miracles or launch global missions
	- St. Thérèse of Lisieux championed "The Little Way", the idea that doing mundane, everyday tasks (like washing dishes, holding your tongue when irritated, or listening patiently) with extraordinary love is what forms a saint
- and we should also remember that we only become good at things through practice
	- and holiness is no different

- each of the Saints had strengths that they are known for
	- St. Francis of Assisi was known for his total trust and reliance on God rather than self-reliance or wealth
		- he gave away his wealth to live in simplicity, relying completely on divine providence rather than material security
	- St Monica sat with sorrow, empathizing deeply with suffering, and offering it to God
		- she poured out years of tearful prayer and patient sorrow over her son Augustine’s erratic life, leading to his ultimate conversion
	- St. Francis de Sales showed quiet, disciplined strength under control, patience in the face of pressure
		- he was known for his profound gentleness and patience
	- St. Teresa of Calcutta (Mother Teresa) is known for practical compassion, relentless forgiveness, and gentle care for the broken
		- she spent her life serving the poorest of the poor on the streets of Kolkata, treating every dying person with tender dignity

- when we look backwards at the defining moments of our lives, it is rarely the easy days that helped us grow
	- if you were to look back and think about those moments where you learned the most about yourself, those moments that you felt helped define your character, it is probably the struggles, the tests of character, and the moments where we felt broken but kept moving forward that made you who you are today
	- and I think it was the same way with the Saints
	- they were broken people who didn't let their failures and challenges stop them
		- they may have stumbled and fell, but they always got back up

- Holiness isn't about doing more things
	- it is really about being intentional in all the things you do
	- it is about choosing character, peace, presence, and mercy in our hearts
	- that will change how we do things and how we live our lives

- you are meant to be you, not a copy of someone else
	- while we can draw inspiration from the saints, trying to imitate a specific 4th-century martyr or 12th-century monk will only feel exhausting and unrealistic
	- as St. Francis de Sales famously put it, "Be who you are, and be that well."
	- Sainthood simply means becoming the fullest, most loving, authentic version of yourself in the midst of your actual daily life

- so, as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, let us think about:
	- that in the Beatitudes, Jesus is simply calling us to be just like Him
		- not by trying to be saintly superheroes, but by living as authentic followers of Christ and sharing God's mercy with the world, one holy moment at a time
	- and how the Saints were human just like us, and that even in the face of adversity, they kept trying, they stayed faithful and they let God take care of the rest
		- we can do that too!
