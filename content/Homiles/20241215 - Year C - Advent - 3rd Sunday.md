# Gospel  

[Lk 3:10-18](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/3?10)

The crowds asked John the Baptist,  
“What should we do?”  
He said to them in reply,  
“Whoever has two cloaks  
should share with the person who has none.   
And whoever has food should do likewise.”  
Even tax collectors came to be baptized and they said to him,  
“Teacher, what should we do?”  
He answered them,   
“Stop collecting more than what is prescribed.”  
Soldiers also asked him,  
“And what is it that we should do?”  
He told them,  
“Do not practice extortion,   
do not falsely accuse anyone,   
and be satisfied with your wages.”

Now the people were filled with expectation,   
and all were asking in their hearts   
whether John might be the Christ.  
John answered them all, saying,   
“I am baptizing you with water,  
but one mightier than I is coming.  
I am not worthy to loosen the thongs of his sandals.  
He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.  
His winnowing fan is in his hand to clear his threshing floor  
and to gather the wheat into his barn,   
but the chaff he will burn with unquenchable fire.”  
Exhorting them in many other ways,   
he preached good news to the people.

# Homily

- One of the ways I have heard Advent described as is a time of 'joyous expectation'
    - Another way of describing this might be 'happily waiting' or waiting in anticipation
    - We wait with patience. We wait to celebrate the birth of our Lord Jesus Christ

- Waiting seems to be a big part of the holiday season
    - Waiting in line at the stores, or waiting in traffic is not the same as waiting to celebrate the birth of Jesus, but both do require patience
    - The word patience comes from the Latin word patior, which means "to suffer"
	    - Now, isn't that interesting
    - So, I guess you could say that "waiting patiently" means suffering through the present moment, in anticipation of something that we are hoping for
    - And I have to admit, patience can be a struggle for me for certain things

- When we are waiting in line at the store, something that happens all too often at this time of year, we need to be patient as well
    - Whenever I am in a store and picking a line to pay my purchases, I pick the one that I think will be the quickest
    - Its funny though as many times, the line I pick, will just as often end up taking longer than the others
    - When this happens, I have to think that God must have a sense of humour. He must be reminding me that I need more practice being patient

- In today's Gospel, we hear some advice from John the Baptist to the people of Jerusalem that might help us to be more patient in our waiting during Advent
    - We always hear a message from John the Baptist during the season of Advent
    - This makes sense, since John the Baptist is the one who goes before the Lord to prepare his way
    - To bring a message of good news and one of forgiveness and repentance to the people, to prepare those who will listen for the arrival of Jesus, our Lord and Saviour

- He also tells us that we must share what we have
    - John says "Whoever has two coats should share with the person who has none. And whoever has food should do likewise"
    - This is very much like what Jesus says in the Sermon on the Mount. To clothe the naked, to feed the hungry
    - Sharing is something taught to us as we grow up
        - For those of us who are parents, sharing is something we deem important to teach to our children at an early age

	- Why does Jesus ask us to share? There are many reasons for this
        - When we share, we are thinking about the needs of the other person and we bring joy to that person as well as to ourselves
        - Sharing helps us to be grateful and helps us to realize how fortunate we are
        - Sharing is an act of love and of sacrifice and can make an impact in our family, in our community and in the world
	- There are many ways of sharing. We all have our own unique gifts and talents to help us to be able to share in ways that help others, and also help you

- Another message that we hear today is that when we are working, we should do the best that we can at our jobs
    - A lot of us spend a good portion of our day working, giving us a great opportunity to use our gifts and talents to help others
    - John didn't ask the tax collector to leave his job
    - Rather, He asked him to work out his own salvation by doing that job as it should be done
    - If you are a tax-collector then be a good tax-collector; If you are a soldier be a good soldier
    - It is a persons duty to serve God where God had placed you. To use our gifts and talents that have been given to us wisely and to help others

- Jesus asks us to do our best at what we do
    - Through our work, we can enrich our spirit knowing that the work that we do, when done well, will help others
    - Through our work, we can also experience joy

- There will be days when we wonder where we will get the strength to do our job well
    - It can be tough at this time of year when there seems to be so much to do
    - And there are other days when we wonder how others are able to do so much
    - That we are able to do what we do though, is possible through the strength we get from the Holy Spirit in our lives
    - There will be times throughout the year when we may forget that, but for me, the peace of the Christmas season is one of those times where I am reminded of how many gifts and blessings that I have
    - I hope that is true for you as well

- That peace comes through the knowledge that God is with us and that Jesus is our savior
    - That peace brings joy, a joy that makes the season so special
    - That peace helps us to bear the waiting that is a part of this season

- Now while I look forward to Christmas each year, not everyone feels the same way
	- I have been leading a grief support group in Hamilton and I know that for some of the people in the group, this Christmas will be a very different Christmas for them
	- For some, this will be their first Christmas without a significant person in their lives to share the holiday with
	- For others, this can be a lonely time of the year
		- For those who have fallen on hard times and may be homeless
		- Or those whose family has moved away and they can't be together through the holidays
	- But despite their situations, my hope is that they will be able to find at least some joy in the holiday season.
	- Maybe it is us who can help them find some joy

- The Advent season is a time of growing and deepening our connection with Jesus
	- Jesus who, by becoming human, became our saviour.  By being born into our world, He made our salvation possible
	- You will find joy when you allow God to change you.  You will find joy in abundance when you embrace Jesus's ways in your life
	- This joy will help you to help others in your life

	- Our God is a God of Faith
	- Our God is a God of Hope
	- Our God is a God of Love
	- If you have Faith, Hope and Love in your life, you will find Joy in your life, even amidst the problems and challenges that each of us face!

- So as we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist, and as we continue to prepare ourselves through Advent, let us think about how we can share the gifts that we have been blessed with
    - Let us think about being patient with ourselves and the people in our lives
    - and also think about the birth of Jesus that we will celebrate on Christmas Day and the joy that our faith brings to our lives today
