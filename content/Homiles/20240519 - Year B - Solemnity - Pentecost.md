# 1st Reading 
### Acts 2.1-11 of Sunday May 19, 2024

When the day of Pentecost had come, they were all together in one place. And suddenly from heaven there came a sound like the rush of a violent wind, and it filled the entire house where they were sitting. Divided tongues, as of fire, appeared among them, and a tongue rested on each of them. All of them were filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other languages, as the Spirit gave them ability.  
  
Now there were devout Jews from every nation under heaven living in Jerusalem. And at this sound the crowd gathered and was bewildered, because each one heard them speaking in their own language. Amazed and astonished, they asked, “Are not all these who are speaking Galileans? And how is it that we hear, each of us, in our own language? Parthians, Medes, Elamites, and residents of Mesopotamia, Judea and Cappadocia, Pontus and Asia, Phrygia and Pamphylia, Egypt and the parts of Libya belonging to Cyrene, and visitors from Rome, both Jews and converts, Cretans and Arabs — in our own languages we hear them speaking about God’s deeds of power.”


# Homily

- Challenge is a gift!!
	- Because, think about it.  If life was easy, what would you learn?
	- I know we all want life to be easy, but haven't we learned more from the challenges we faced in our lives?
	- Now, it might not always feel like a gift when you are in the midst of the challenge
		- especially one you didn't ask for
		- but looking backwards, looking at the challenges I have faced in my life
		- I have found that the challenges that I have faced have presented me with the opportunity to grow and use the gifts that God has given me
		- So maybe God's plan gives us what we need at the right time so that we are prepared for the next challenge on our path

- Today we celebrate Pentecost.
	- In the first reading from Acts, we hear how the Apostle's received the Holy Spirit
	- An exercise I sometimes like to do is after reading a Scripture passage, is to close my eyes and picture what is happening in the reading as if I was there
	- Basically, trying to immerse myself in the passage as a 1st person witness
	- This is one of those readings that I think is a good one to try this with
	- Think about it:
		- All of a sudden, hearing a sound from heaven, like a rush of violent wind
		- Filling the room that you are sitting in
		- Then seeing tongues of fire around you, moving to rest on everyone in the room
		- The Holy Spirit resting on each of the Apostles!!
		- Can you imagine?
		- How would you react?
		- Would you be scared?  Amazed about what was going on?  Excited?
	- And then, seeing the Apostles being able to speak in the different languages of the people in the city
		- so that the people in Jerusalem could hear the Word of God in their own language
	- What an amazing gift that must have been.  Both for the Apostles and for those who listened to them!
	- Can you imagine the joy the Apostles must have felt, to receive the Holy Spirit?

- I think I can sort of understand
	- it reminds me of when I received my calling to become a Permanent Deacon
	- it happened in the middle of the night, sometime around 2am
	- no, I didn't see any tongues of fire
	- but I woke up and couldn't get back to sleep, so I started thinking about where my life was going
	- and one of the things that I was thinking about was a feeling that was pulling me to think about becoming a Deacon
	- and that night, I didn't know what the next step was, but I decided that this was a path I needed to follow
	- I said yes to that path
	- and after I said yes, I felt this sense of peace and joy unlike anything I have ever felt in my life before or since
	- I can't explain it any other way
	- and following that path has been a challenge for me, and yet, it has also been a gift for me and has changed me in ways I could not have imagined

- Pentecost Sunday always reminds me of the Sacrament of Confirmation
	- In the Sacrament of Baptism, we enter into the family of the Church, becoming adopted children of God
		- we are cleansed from Original Sin and receive sanctifying grace  and the Holy Spirit to help us on our spiritual journey
	- In the Sacrament of Communion, we receive spiritual nourishment to sustain us on our journey
		- It helps to unite us with Christ in the sacrifice He made for our salvation
	- In the Sacrament of Confirmation, the Confirmandi receive sanctifying grace and the Holy Spirit to strengthen them on their journey
		- And they receive the gifts of the Holy Spirit to help them in their day to day lives
	- Last week in his Homily, Deacon Joe talked about 3 of the gifts of the Holy Spirit
	- He talked about knowledge, understanding and wisdom
	- The other 4 gifts of the Holy Spirit are counsel, fortitude, piety and the fear of God

- I don't remember much about my own Confirmation, other than the Bishop questioning the Confirmandi about the gifts of the Holy Spirit in his Homily
	- I wouldn't even have been able to tell you what date my Confirmation was on until I had to gather that information for my application to become a Permanent Deacon
	- My Confirmation took place on June 2nd, 1981
	- Yes, that was a lot of years ago!
	- I do feel though that I do have in at least some measure,  each of the gifts of the Holy Spirit as a part of me
	- Some, definitely are stronger than others
	- I have always had a thirst for learning and using what I learn, so knowledge and understanding are gifts that resonate with me
	- Wisdom?  Hmm, not sure on that one, but maybe to a small degree

- That is one of the things though about our gifts
	- If we invite the Holy Spirit into our lives
	- If we use our gifts in our lives, helping others where we can
	- Our gifts will grow and multiply
	- That's not to say that everything will go the way we want it to though
	- There is a quote from Mark Twain that goes:
		- Good decisions come from experience
		- Experience comes from bad decisions
	- So we are due to make some bad choices in our lives
	- But if we can make the Holy Spirit a part of the decision process, if we use our gifts where we can, then I have to think we are more likely to grow our gifts
	- And I don't know about you, but for me, I feel like the best version of myself when I am using my gifts, especially to help others

- While I don't remember a lot about my Confirmation, I do remember a lot about my Ordination to the Permanent Diaconate
	- It was on June 2nd, 2018
	- And one of the things I learned and continue to learn as I follow the path that God has made for me is that:
		- when we allow the Holy Spirit to enter us and to guide us, the Spirit does not take over,
		- rather He enriches our lives in ways that we could never expect!
	- There were many challenges along the journey, and there have been times when I have had doubts about whether becoming a Deacon was the right path for me
	- But I am thinking that therein lies the gift part of challenge
	- The challenges that we face in our life, can either cause us to doubt ourselves and our abilities and our gifts,
		- or they can give us the opportunity to embrace that challenge using our gifts to become a better version of ourselves

- When I think about the Apostles, I remember that they were chosen by Jesus to follow Him
	- Yet, they still had to make their own decision to follow Jesus
	- Jesus who chose them to follow the path that God had made for them
	- And although the Apostles faced many challenges on their journey, when you are doing what you are meant to be doing, you will find joy in your life

- Jesus calls each of us to serve
	- To follow the path that God has made for us
	- And it is with the gifts that the Holy Spirit has given us that will help us move forward on that path

- So here's the challenge for you
- As we prepare to celebrate the Eucharist this Pentecost, let us
	- open our hearts to the Holy Spirit, like the Apostles did on the first Pentecost
	- think about how can you use the gifts the Holy Spirit has given you to face the challenges ahead on your path
	- think about how we can thank God for the gifts he has given us
