# Gospel

## Luke 10.38-42 of Sunday July 20, 2025

Now as Jesus and his disciples went on their way, he entered a certain village, where a woman named Martha welcomed him into her home. She had a sister named Mary, who sat at the Lord’s feet and listened to what he was saying.  
  
But Martha was distracted by her many tasks; so she came to Jesus and asked, “Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to do all the work by myself? Tell her then to help me.”  
  
But the Lord answered her, “Martha, Martha, you are worried and distracted by many things; there is need of only one thing. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken away from her.”


## Homily

- Where your attention goes, your energy flows
	- this is a common phrase that I've both heard before and seen on different social media posts
	- And there is certainly some truth to this message
	- What you focus on and think about, 
		- the things you prioritize in your life, 
		- these tend to be the things that your time gets spent on
	- And time is one of those things that you really can't make more of
	- There are only 24 hours in a day
	- You can't add more time to a day
	- The expression "you can't make time, you can only take it" sums it up pretty well
	- And if you have heard yourself using that phrase "there are not enough hours in the day" as I know I have in the past, 
		- then you are probably in a situation where your focus is not on the things that matter the most
	- When I hear myself using this phrase, it's a reminder to think about what's taking my time now
		- What am I saying yes to by default?
		- What distractions are catching my attention and taking me away from my priorities?
		- Am I clear on what my priorities are?
		- Or do I need to take some time to re-evaluate that?
	- Now granted, there are times when a whole bunch of problems seem to land on my plate all at once
		- but like anything else in life, some things are more important than others
		- and if you can take some time to figure out what the most important things are
		- then you usually can figure out what needs your time now and what can be done later
	- Hence, what I've learned over time is to try to find balance in my day and in my week
	- Taking the time to figure out what the priorities are now that my time should be spent on and what can wait is a choice that I have to make
		- and then following through on that is another choice I have to make
	- And full disclosure here, I don't always succeed at this, but I keep trying
		- with experience and some time to think, I find over time, I am getting better at it

- In today's Gospel, we hear the story of Jesus visiting the house of Martha and Mary
	- It's Martha who welcomes Jesus to their house, but Mary is the one who sits at His feet, listening and learning from Him
	- Martha becomes distracted by her tasks and duties, offering hospitality to their guest
	- Then after, Martha complains to Jesus, that she needs help, that Mary should be helping her instead of spending her time with Jesus
	- Martha complains to Jesus, asking Him to tell Mary to help
		- Martha feels that all the work has fallen onto her
	- But Jesus says to her
		- “Martha, Martha, you are worried and distracted by many things; there is need of only one thing. Mary has chosen the better part, which will not be taken away from her.”
		- Let's think about what Jesus really meant here

- One of the most obvious thoughts for me that comes to mind is that of where your focus, your presence is directed
	- Beyond preparing meals, true hospitality involves offering our presence and attention to others, especially to Christ who comes to us in various forms
	- Martha and Mary have Jesus visit their home, and of course hospitality is important
	- but does our hospitality focus merely on the physical aspects
		- is our focus on preparing the food, making sure others are comfortable?
		- sure these things are important, but if all our time is spent on those aspects, 
			- to the point where our focus is not on our guest, are we truly offering hospitality?
		- hospitality involves extending warmth, kindness, and a welcoming atmosphere, making others feel comfortable and valued
		- It's about showing respect and providing for their needs
	- In the first reading, we see another example of hospitality, in the case of Abraham welcoming three men.
		- He took care of the preparations and welcoming his guests, but he also sat with them while they ate, being present with them
		- Abraham's spontaneous, generous hospitality leads to the promise of a son, a miraculous fulfillment.
	- Martha was distracted by her tasks and was not present with Jesus in the same way Mary was

- Another interesting thought for me was thinking about the aspects of service and spirituality as reflected by Martha and Mary
	- in our faith, both service and spirituality are important
	- in some parts of Scripture, there is a clear message that Jesus calls us to serve others, to bring God to those we meet in our day and serving others is an important way of doing this
	- but in today's Gospel, Jesus also highlights the importance of spirituality
	- Martha was busy with aspects of service while Mary was focused on spirituality
	- Although Jesus said that Mary chose the better part
		- choosing the spiritual aspect while Martha chose service
		- it's not an "either/or" situation
		- it's more of a "both/and" situation
		- Jesus calls us to focus on both serving others and to embrace our spirituality
	- there is a balance needed between the spiritual side of life and the service side of life
		- service requires sacrifice and vulnerability
		- opening our homes and hearts to others, even Christ, requires vulnerability and a willingness to be interrupted
			- a willingness to have our own priorities set aside to allow us to focus on others
		- Jesus provides many examples in Scripture of His being vulnerable and sacrificing His needs for others, as exemplified by His dying on the Cross for our salvation
	- True service requires a foundation in spirituality

- And one last thought to share
	- In our lives, or at least in mine, there is no shortage of things to do
		- both things that need to be done, but also things I want to do
		- and then there are all the distractions that take our focus away from the things that we need and want to do
	- but we can't be busy all the time
	- we need to take time to rest and recharge
		- Jesus invites us to lay down our burdens and find rest in His presence, as Mary did. This isn't laziness but spiritual wisdom

- So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist, let us think about
	- where our is attention going?
	- is our service to others rooted in the spiritual side of our life?
	- are we reaching out to Jesus to help with that?
