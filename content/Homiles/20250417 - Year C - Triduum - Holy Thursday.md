## Gospel

### John 13.1-15 of Thursday April 17, 2025

Before the festival of the Passover, Jesus knew that his hour had come to depart from this world and go to the Father. Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.  
  
The devil had already put it into the heart of Judas, son of Simon Iscariot, to betray him. And during supper Jesus, knowing that the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he had come from God and was going to God, got up from the table, took off his outer robe, and tied a towel around himself. Then he poured water into a basin and began to wash the disciples’ feet and to wipe them with the towel that was tied around him.  
  
He came to Simon Peter, who said to him, “Lord, are you going to wash my feet?” Jesus answered, “You do not know now what I am doing, but later you will understand.” Peter said to him, “You will never wash my feet.” Jesus answered, “Unless I wash you, you have no share with me.” Simon Peter said to him, “Lord, not my feet only but also my hands and my head!” Jesus said to him, “One who has bathed does not need to wash, except for the feet, but is entirely clean. And you are clean, though not all of you.” For he knew who was to betray him; for this reason he said, “Not all of you are clean.”  
  
After he had washed their feet, put on his robe, and returned to the table, Jesus said to them, “Do you know what I have done to you? You call me Teacher and Lord — and you are right, for that is what I am. So if I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another’s feet. For I have set you an example, that you also should do as I have done to you.”

## Homily


- Everyone loves to receive gifts.  
	- If you look at the definition of gift, one of those definitions is the following:
		- something voluntarily given by one person to another without compensation
		- Tonight's Gospel opens our eyes to 3 gifts given that Jesus gives us at the Last Supper.
			- The Institution of the Holy Eucharist
			- The Ordained Priesthood
			- and the Lord's command to love one another.

- During Lent:
	- We have reflected on our sinfulness, looked at how blessed we are to receive the gifts we have from God, even if we are unworthy.
	- We as a Church have reflected upon the humanity of Christ.
	- In our liturgy, through our focus on prayer, fasting, and alms giving.
	- Through participating in the Stations of the Cross.
	- And most particularly in our consideration of the Passion of Christ.
	- It prepares us for the Sacred Pascal Triduum which starts tonight with Holy Thursday


- **Eucharist**

- The Eucharist is that gift which can lift us up from the many things that bog us down in our day to day lives and remind us that there is something more, a greater path for us to follow.
- And this helps us to step back and renew our focus on those things that are more important in our lives.

- Jesus knew what was coming in His own life and that the disciples would need something to carry them through the turbulent and challenging times they would face after His Death and Resurrection.
- The Eucharist is that which helped them to regain their focus, to know that there was a larger purpose than their own wants and needs.
- It helped them to forge ahead in the face of the trials they themselves would face.
- And it is there for us as well, to help us to move forward through the trials we ourselves will and have faced in our lives.
- The Eucharist, the bread and wine, that after consecration becomes the body and blood of Christ, in our midst, as a gift from God.
	- For us to share in, to strengthen us, to comfort us, to give us the hope of a future with our God.


- **Priestly Order**

- The gift of the priesthood also brings us many gifts that affect our lives.
- Those who serve as priests use their gifts to bring us spiritual leadership, consolation in challenging times, and spiritual healing to name but a few gifts.
- They use their priestly gifts to serve as Jesus served
- But one important gift that the priesthood brings us as well is the ability to celebrate Mass, to bring the Eucharist to us, the children of God.
- To bring to us the Eucharist, through the consecration of the bread and wine, the real presence of Jesus in the form of the body and blood of Christ.
- There is a lot more I could say about the gift of the priesthood, but then that wouldn't leave much time to talk about the 3rd gift I want to mention tonight as well.


- **Fraternal Charity**

- That 3rd gift is the Lords' command to love one another.

- In tonight's Gospel, Jesus washes the feet of His disciples.
	- This is a task normally done by a slave, considered below someone of Jesus' stature.
	- But Jesus dressed like a slave, washes the feet of His disciples.
	- He serves them, to show them that service is love.
	- He models humility, a theme we have seen in many places throughout the Gospels.
	- Through His humility, Jesus shows the disciples the importance of showing love to one another, in this case through His service to them.
	- He shows them this in His last hours before He begins His Passion so that the disciples with time, might see that His own upcoming sacrifice on the Cross is also an act of service, an act of love for their and for all of our salvation.

- There are many definitions of love, but in particular, I like this one:
	- To love is to will the good of the other as other - not to will my good through the other, but truly to want and will what is good for the other
	- Jesus wanted what was best for God the Father, for His disciples, and for all of us.
	- He wanted that enough, that He gave all.

- So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist,
	- Let us think about 3 of the gifts that in particular we celebrate tonight:
		- The Institution of the Holy Eucharist
		- The Ordained Priesthood
		- and the Lord's command to love one another
	- What impact have these gifts had and will continue to have in our lives.
