## Gospel

### John 13.1, 31-33a, 34-35 of Sunday May 18, 2025

Before the festival of the Passover, Jesus knew that his hour had come to depart from this world and go to the Father. Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end.  
  
During the supper, when Judas had gone out, Jesus said, “Now the Son of Man has been glorified, and God has been glorified in him. If God has been glorified in him, God will also glorify him in himself and will glorify him at once.  
  
“Little children, I am with you only a little longer. I give you a new commandment, that you love one another. Just as I have loved you, you also should love one another. By this everyone will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another.”


## Homily

- When I was thinking about how to start this Homily, my original thought was to start it by saying "Go in peace, glorifying the Lord by your life", 
	- you know, the dismissal that I often use at the end of Mass
	- but then, if everyone got up and left, well, that wouldn't be good would it?
	- but this dismissal that I use at the end of Mass, it aligns with today's Gospel quite a bit, so let's think about that a bit more

- In today's Gospel, Jesus gives the disciples a new commandment:
	- "love one another.  As I have loved you, so you also should love one another"
	- He then goes on to say "This is how all will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another"
	- Let's unpack these points in a bit more detail so that we can understand it better

- Now firstly, what is different about this commandment, what makes it new?
	- Well, in Leviticus in the Old Testament, it says "You shall love your neighbor as yourself"
	- A good rule to follow
	- However, what Jesus told the disciples was to love others as I have loved you
	- Now, let us think, does that describe how we love our neighbours?

- The way that Jesus loves us, it is not fleeting
	- when we get distracted with the details of our lives and focus only on our own wants and needs, when we neglect our relationship with Jesus, Jesus doesn't stop loving us
	- He is always there, waiting for us to come back to Him
	- Jesus modelled the way He wants us to love others
	- Jesus' love is self-sacrificing and unconditional
	- He didn't shy away from talking to those who lived on the margins of society, those who were outcast and shunned
	- He helped those in need, healed those He could
	- And ultimately, He loved us enough that He gave up His life for us by suffering on the Cross so that He could be Resurrected for our salvation
	- Can we say that about our love of our neighbor?

- And just who are we referring to when we use the term "neighbor",
- We are in some way connected to everyone around us, through our connections to others in our families, in our parish, in our communities, in our country, and ultimately, in our world.
	- And nothing highlight's this more these days than Social Media
	- I don't spend as much time on social media as I used to, but one of the things I have noticed is that most Social Media platforms are always suggesting people who you might know to connect with through the friends and connections you already have on their platform
	- I had an example recently where I had met someone new through a friend and a week or so later, they popped up as someone I might know on Instagram.
	- And as I was looking at their profile, it listed people that we were both connected to, and I came to find out that this person who I had just met a couple of weeks ago is also connected to a family member of mine that I didn't know about  
	- So, it really highlights how interconnected our social circles are; 
		- people we know often know each other too

- And why might Jesus want us to love others as Jesus loves us?
	- Jesus, being the Son of God, perfectly embodies God's love
	- His love is unconditional, self-sacrificing, merciful, and persistent
	- By loving others as Jesus loves us, we are reflecting the very nature of God into the world. 
		- We become living examples of divine love in action
	- Maybe, our purpose here on Earth is to learn a collective lesson in love
		- each of us matters greatly to other people and we all have our place in the universe
		- we are all born worthy as children of God
		- and we are all capable of great love
		- we all have the opportunity to shine our light, to share our gifts with those around us
		- it is up to us to embrace our gifts and let them shine in this world
		- it is up to each of us to love one another as Jesus loves us

- Now, I read something that other day that, at least in my mind, connects with today's Gospel
	- It goes "to love others is to risk discomfort"
	- when we hear to the term love, we mostly think about the positive sides of love
		- the gratitude and thanks we might receive from someone that we did an act of kindness for
		- the warmth we feel when we spend time with someone we care about
	- We often don't think about the vulnerability that is required to love thy neighbour as Jesus does
		- To love requires us to make ourselves vulnerable
		- Not everyone is going to react warmly to a kind gesture
		- or maybe our kind gesture is not perceived as being kind by the recipient
		- helping and supporting others can be time-consuming, emotionally draining, and might require us to put our own needs aside
		- it's often easier to remain in our comfort zone than to actively engage in acts of service driven by love
	- and when we think about those who are closer to us, those we care about a lot, there is a risk of losing them
	- I volunteer regular working with people who are bereaved, who are dealing with the loss of a significant person in their lives
		- this is not always easy and this work can be draining, but at the same time, as I work with them, I can see the changes in their lives and I am energized by this as I feel this is a part of my purpose and mission
		- now one of the things I have found that really makes a difference in their lives is connecting with others who are also experiencing a significant loss in their own lives
		- this helps them know that even though they may have lost a significant person in their lives, someone they have loved, they are not alone
		- often, people who have met as total strangers at the first session in the grief program end up being friends and stay connected after the program finishes
		- even though they are in the midst of dealing with the loss of a loved one, they are helping others in the same situation by loving their neighbor as Jesus has loved us

- So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist,
	- let us think about how you can "Go in peace, glorifying the Lord with your life"
	- and remember, as children of God, we are all capable of great love
	- so, when you leave after Mass today, let us bring that great love to others so that people will know we are disciples of Jesus
