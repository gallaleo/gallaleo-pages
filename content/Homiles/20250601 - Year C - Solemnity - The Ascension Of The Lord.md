## Gospel

## Luke 24.46-53 of Sunday June 1, 2025

Jesus said to the disciples, “Thus it is written, that the Christ is to suffer and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins is to be proclaimed in his name to all nations, beginning from Jerusalem. You are witnesses of these things.  
  
“And see, I am sending upon you what my Father promised; so stay here in the city until you have been clothed with power from on high.”  
  
Then he led them out as far as Bethany, and, lifting up his hands, he blessed them. While he was blessing them, he withdrew from them and was carried up into heaven. And they worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy; and they were continually in the temple blessing God.

## Homily

- I sometimes find it interesting to look deeper into the definitions of both new words and words that we commonly use as you may have noticed that I do as part of my Homilies from time to time
	- and there is actually a word to describe people who do that
	- the term is "logophile"
	- someone who enjoys learning new words, delving into the meanings of words and their origins

- I have sometimes found as well that although we may use a word frequently, when asked to define said word, it is not as easy as you would think to describe what the word means
	- For example, if you were asked to give a definition of the word love, what would you say?
	- Defining "love" is notoriously difficult because it's a complex, multifaceted concept experienced in countless ways, across various types of relationships, and with different intensities
	- It encompasses a vast range of emotions, behaviors, and beliefs
	- Before studying the word, I would have thought of it as a emotion, but it is more than that
	- We might describe it as:
		- A strong feeling of affection which is a common thread across most forms of love, whether for a person, animal, or even an object or idea.
		- we might describe it as a deep emotional attachment
		-  or maybe we describe it as a benevolent concern for the good or welfare of another
		- A desire for the well-being, happiness, and flourishing of the beloved often implying a degree of self-sacrifice or prioritizing the other's needs

- Jesus's love for us is often described as an unconditional, self-sacrificing, and redemptive love
	- Jesus, God, became man
	- God, who is infinite and all-powerful, chose to become finite and vulnerable by taking on human flesh in Jesus Christ
	- Not as an observer in our world, but as a person who entered into our human condition, experiencing its joys, sorrows, temptations, and limitations
	- He came to teach us the way to eternal life
	- He brought compassion and healing, not just for the elite, but to all, and especially to those who were vulnerable
		- the sick, the needy, the outcasts
	- He exemplified love through humble service, most strikingly by washing the feet of his disciples at the Last Supper
	- He made the ultimate sacrifice for us:
		- dying on the Cross for our sins
		- to overcome death through the Resurrection
	- Jesus's love conquers even death itself

- Today, we are celebrating the Ascension of the Lord
	- We are celebrating Jesus's return to the Father after He has been Resurrected from the Dead
	- We are celebrating Jesus returning home

- Home is another complex word when you look at what it means
	- The definition of "home" encompasses both tangible and intangible aspects. 
	- When we hear the word home, we most often think of its meaning as a place where someone lives, like a house or an apartment or condo
	- But it can also refer to a place of origin.
		- I often still refer to  Prince Edward Island as **back home** even though I have lived in Ontario for 25 years
	- It can also mean a base of operations, like a home office or a home base
	- It can also be a place of affection or belonging
		- a place of comfort, where one feels safe and secure, where a sense of belonging is felt
	- I have to think that this last meaning is how Jesus felt when He returned home to the Father

- But just because Jesus has Ascended to heaven, just because He has returned home to the Father, that does not mean that his love for us stopped
	- And it also does not mean that He has abandoned us either.

- If you recall, in the Gospel Acclamation that was sung before I proclaimed the Gospel today, it says:
	- "I am with you always, til the end of the age"
	- He doesn't say "I will be with you always",
	- He says "I **AM** with you always"
	- This comes from the concluding promise of Jesus to his disciples, found in the Gospel of Matthew

- Jesus also promises to be with us in other ways.
	- He promises us that He will not leave us alone, that the Holy Spirit will be with us, to strengthen us, to guide us along our journey
	- He is also with us and is present in the Eucharist which we consume when we partake of it here at Mass
	- Our journey of faith gives us the hope that we too will return to the Father, that we to will return home to be with Jesus

- Now, thinking about the disciples, it would be totally understandable if they were feeling sad and alone after seeing Jesus rise to heaven
	- They loved Jesus and Jesus loved them
	- Jesus has recently died on the Cross, then they saw Him after he was Resurrected
		- It would be understandable for them to feel like they were on an emotional roller coater ride
	- as with any challenge we face in our lives though, our attitude has a lot to do with how we proceed forward after being faced with a change or a challenge
	- In this case, the disciples were celebrating with joy!
	- They knew Jesus was going home to the Father
	- And they knew that they would some day join Jesus once their own journeys were completed
	- They knew that Jesus was leaving them with a final gift, that of the Holy Spirit
	- and it is through the power of the Holy Spirit that we can find the hope needed to persevere in the challenges and changes we face in our lives, just like the disciples did
	- all we have to do is to accept the Holy Spirit into our lives
	- to let the Spirit guide us, in the decisions we face, helping us to move forward

- The challenges and changes we face in our life can feel like a burden or sometimes even overwhelm us
	- but through the help of the Holy Spirit, those challenges and changes can give us an opportunity to explore our faith in new ways, 
	- to gain a new awareness of ourselves and our capabilities, 
	- and through the lens of perspective, see ourselves in new and different ways

- The Ascension is a reminder that Christ has overcome everything and has given us the Holy Spirit to help us do the same

- So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist,
	- let us think about what the Ascension teaches us
	- about how the Holy Spirit can help us through the challenges and changes we face in our lives
	- and how our faith and hope and the love of Jesus can give us the strength we need to continue our journey with that same joy that the disciples had
