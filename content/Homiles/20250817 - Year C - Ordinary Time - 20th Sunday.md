# Gospel

## Luke 12.49-53

Jesus said to his disciples: “I came to bring fire to the earth, and how I wish it were already kindled! I have a baptism with which to be baptized, and what stress I am under until it is completed!  
  
“Do you think that I have come to bring peace to the earth? No, I tell you, but rather division! From now on five in one household will be divided, three against two and two against three; they will be divided: father against son and son against father, mother against daughter and daughter against mother, mother-in-law against her daughter-in-law and daughter-in-law against mother-in-law.”


# Homily

- I read something the other day that I felt was worth sharing with others
	- There was a priest who had arrived early for Mass, vested in Green and hoping for some silence, a few sacred moments alone with God, before Mass started
	- He went to a quiet corner of the Church to pray
	- Then a child came up to him asking for a blessing
	- then another child came up to him
	- then two more children came up to him
	- and before he knew it, he was surround by 4 kids aged from 4 to 7 years old
	- He gladly gave a blessing to each of the children
	- But then they stayed, talking, dancing about, praying
	- one boy hugged him and shouted out "you have a big tummy" loud enough for everyone to hear
	- they were adorable, but truthfully, he just wanted his solitude back
- but then something happened
	- one of the children said to him "Father, I want to bless you"
	- and finally, he realized what was happening
	- God was reaching out to him in a different way
	- God was giving him a sacred visit, a holy encounter, a divine disturbance
	- because sometimes, that's what God does
	- Yes, His presence provides comfort, but it can also interrupt us
	- it can shake up our schedules and our plans
- sometimes, He comes to us in Scripture or in a Sacrament
	- other times, in traffic, in illness, in a child's sticky hands
	- In noise, in chaos, in questions and even in people we didn't pan for
	- And sometimes, we miss experiencing God, not because we don't want to see Him
	- And not because He isn't near
	- But because we don't want to be disturbed, we don't want to adjust our plans
- We all want to have God in our lives, and God wants that too
	- But, if you want to walk with God, you have to let Him interrupt your life
	- Because sometimes grace doesn't knock
	- It barges in...  laughing, loud, persistent, and full of love
- the priest realized afterwards that he almost missed an encounter with God because he was too busy praying
	- so, next time your plans are disturbed, look again
	- it might just be God looking to bless you

- Jesus said "I came to bring fire to the earth." in today's Gospel
    - In Scripture, fire as a symbol has more than one meaning
    - It is a biblical symbol for purification but also for judgement
    - Fire can be helpful, like for cooking, or for warmth
    - Fire can also be seen as destructive
	    - Like how a forest fire destroys the trees and plants in a forest and anything else in its' path
        - But even after all the destruction, there is a possibility of rebirth and renewal, like when  new growth springs up to replace that which was lost
    - Fire is also a symbol of the Holy Spirit
        - On the Road to Emmaus, after the two disciples talked to the risen Jesus, they described their hearts as "burning within us"
        - And at Pentecost, the Holy Spirit descended on the Apostles as tongues of fire
        - They were filled with passion and zeal, the Holy Spirit alive within them.
    - When Jesus says He came to bring fire to the earth,
        - Was he foretelling of bringing the Holy Spirit to His disciples and to us?
        - The Holy Spirit that we receive through our Baptism?

- Or maybe the fire was meant to disrupt us, to burn away our resistance to the path that God has planned for us
	- Like when we stay in our comfortable routines, avoiding pushing ourselves to do something we should because it feels too difficult to try something new?
	- And when it comes to living our faith and life in general, sometimes we resist the changes that are put in front of us, even if those changes are intended to help us grow
		- Living our faith will challenge us
		- And it won't always be easy
		- there will be times involving intense challenge, criticism, or even a severe test of character
		- sometimes though, resisting the change in front of us causes more friction in our lives than accepting the change and then moving on to figuring out how to move forward

- Jesus speaks of both fire and baptism
- At our Baptism, we have been called to mission, to live our purpose
	- Finding our mission, our purpose, is possible only if we are willing to look beyond ourselves
	- We need to embrace our faith and let that guide us
	- Mission is not only driven by our own desire to do something, be something, or have something, though certainly that desire may play a role
	- It is driven by the needs of others and the needs of the world 
	- Mission is a meeting between self and service
	- And when we find our mission, we will feel a fire or passion in our purpose
		- our mission gives us the meaning we seek in our lives
	- that's not to say we won't be met with challenge
		- even when we are living our purpose, we will find conflict, division, challenge
		- but our faith can give us the strength to push forward through the challenge

- God's presence in our life can bring us comfort
	- sometimes though, that presence can interrupt our plans, 
		- shake up our life, 
		- take us out of our comfort zone, 
		- not by our choice but by circumstances beyond our control
	- sometimes, it brings conflict or division or challenge to push us to try something we would not otherwise have done
	- taking us along a different path
	- but when God does this in our lives, there is always a purpose, one that may not become clearer til much later in our lives
	- know though that He always has a reason
	- so, when Jesus speaks of division, I have to think this might be one of the things He means

- So, as we continue our celebration and prepare to receive the Eucharist, 
	- let us think about our mission and purpose, that which lights a fire in our soul
	- remember that He wants to be with us, to be included in our lives
	- and when we find interruption or disruption in our lives
		- think whether this might be God trying to guide us down a different path
